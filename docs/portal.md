# Using Kelvin

The portal is where you chat with Kelvin: the desktop app's window, or the site that `kelvin` opens in your browser.

## Signing in

Everyone who uses Kelvin on a lab computer has an account, and everyone is equal: there is no administrator. A lab can set an owner's PIN that guards limits, auto mode and unlocking Hands on the sample ([safety.md](safety.md#the-lab-owners-pin)), and on top of it give people roles: owner, operator and trainee ([safety.md](safety.md#lab-roles)). Anyone at the computer can add themselves with **Add a person** on the sign-in screen; with roles, a new account is a trainee until the owner gives it a role.

- **Yours alone:** your settings and API keys, ChatGPT sign-in, Kelvin hub connection, chats, projects and memory, your data folder (runs and reports), your queue, the sample you mounted, and your record (`events.jsonl`).
- **Everyone's:** the instruments, the lab config, the instrument cards, the lab's memory, the switches for setting up instruments and phone access, and the lab's **instrument log** (`~/Kelvin/instrument-log.jsonl`): every command and approval from everyone, with the name of whoever was signed in. The Lab panel's **Activity → Everyone** shows it.
- **Passwords are optional.** With one, your API keys, ChatGPT sign-in and Kelvin hub token are encrypted with it. Nobody can reset a forgotten password, because there is no administrator; your runs and reports are still in your data folder. Change or remove it in Settings → **Your account**.
- **One person, no password:** Kelvin signs you in by itself when it starts, so a single user never sees the sign-in screen.
- **Signing in while the instruments are set up:** Kelvin opens at once and connects the lab's instruments in the background, which can take minutes (a slow instrument, a maker's driver, reading every setting). You can sign in meanwhile: the sign-in screen says what the setup is doing. Then **Setting up your instruments** shows each step (which instrument it is connecting, then reading their settings) until they are ready, and closes by itself. **Use Kelvin meanwhile** closes it; Kelvin uses each instrument once it is connected.
- **Signing out** (bottom of the sidebar) declines open approvals and turns the approval mode back to asking: one person's yes is never another's. A run or queue you leave running goes on, and is recorded as yours. Nobody else can sign in until it ends; anyone can press **Stop it** on the sign-in screen.
- **Lab roles and the lab config's history** (Settings → **Instruments**): the owner, signed in, gives each person a role with **Give roles…** and the PIN, and with **Where my notifications go** has every safety change sent where their own notifications go, whoever is signed in. **Lab config history…** (also **History…** in Lab setup) lists every version of the lab config and the lab's cards, and each change to who may do what, with who, when and why; **Diff** shows what changed, **Roll back to this…** puts a version back. With roles, a trainee who approves a request is asked who confirms it: an operator or the owner picks their name and types their password, or the owner types the PIN ([safety.md](safety.md#lab-roles)).
- **Before accounts:** the first account takes over what Kelvin kept until then: chats and runs in `~/Kelvin/virtual-lab`, and the API keys.
- **What accounts don't do:** they keep people's work apart inside Kelvin, but they don't lock files. Anyone who can open this computer's files can read the folders.
- **Removing someone from this computer:** before anyone signs in, **Remove someone from this computer** on the sign-in screen lists every account here (old ones from before Kelvin accounts too); signed in, Settings → **Account** → **Delete this account…** does the same for you. It takes that person's password (none for an account without one). It removes what uninstalling Kelvin would remove for them: settings and API keys, the ChatGPT sign-in (revoked with OpenAI first), chats, projects and memory. Their measurements stay: runs, reports and record, in their data folder. A new account never gets their folder, nor what Kelvin kept before accounts if they had taken it over. Refused while a plan or the queue runs.

Each person's things are in `~/Kelvin/users/<name>/`: `settings.json`, `chatgpt.json`, `hub-sync.json` (what was shared on the Kelvin hub), `chats/` and `data/` (their default data folder; Settings → **Data folder** chooses another).

### Kelvin accounts: one account, every computer

People sign in with a **Kelvin account**: email and password, the same on every computer in every lab (`central.py`). There is one [Kelvin hub](hub.md) for every Kelvin user, built into Kelvin (`central.DEFAULT_HUB`: `kelvin.tail269ee5.ts.net` for now, until it moves to its permanent server). It keeps only the accounts. Chats, data and API keys stay on each computer, as above.

- **Create a Kelvin account** on the sign-in screen: name, email, a password of 8 characters or more, and agreeing to the hub's terms and privacy notice. The hub mails a link to confirm the address.
- **First sign-in on a computer:** Kelvin asks the hub. If the computer has accounts from before, Kelvin asks whether one of them is yours. That one (with its password on this computer, if it had one) then keeps its chats, data and API keys. **None of these: start fresh** makes a new folder.
- **Offline:** after one sign-in on a computer, Kelvin keeps a hash of the password there, so the same password signs you in while the hub can't be reached.
- **Changing your password:** Settings → **Account**, while Kelvin reaches the hub. It changes on the hub, and your keys here are encrypted again with the new one. On another computer, the new password works the next time it reaches the hub. Your keys there were encrypted with the old one, so Kelvin asks for it once (**Keep my keys**). **Skip** leaves them unreadable; add them again in Settings.
- **Forgot your password?** On the sign-in screen: the hub mails a link to choose a new one. The old one still opens a computer that can't reach the hub, until it does.
- **Name, email confirmation, export:** on your account page on the hub (Settings → **Account** → **Your account on the Kelvin hub**).
- **Deleting your Kelvin account:** tick **Also delete my Kelvin account, on every computer** when you remove it (above), or use **Delete account** on your account page on the hub. The hub checks the password and forgets the account; this computer then removes it too. Other computers keep your account there, unable to sign in, until it is removed there as well. Without the hub, remove it from this computer only.
- **One hub for everyone:** there is nothing to set up per lab. The sign-in screen and Settings → **Account** show the hub. `account_hub` in `~/Kelvin/settings.json` names another, only for development. Signed out, it can only change until someone has signed in on this computer with a Kelvin account, so nobody can point the sign-in screen at a hub of their own.
- **Your phone, with no pairing:** sign in to Kelvin for iPhone with the same Kelvin account. While you are signed in to a Kelvin computer, your phone reaches it through the hub, from any network ([hub.md](hub.md#phones-through-the-hub)). Unshared work (unpublished, confidential or model-limited projects, incognito chats and their runs) doesn't go through the hub; it opens when the phone reaches the computer directly. Settings → **Account** says whether your phone can reach this Kelvin now, and switches it off.
- **For now** Kelvin shares no work with the hub: Settings → **Account** has no hub connection, and projects have no hub setting (`HUB_SHARING` in `web/app.py`).

## In the portal

- **Text size:** Settings → **Display** makes the whole Kelvin window bigger or smaller (80% to 200%), as do Ctrl + and Ctrl −, or Ctrl and the mouse wheel; Ctrl 0 goes back to 100%. It is yours: it follows you when you sign in on that computer. In a browser, use the browser's own zoom.

- **Approval cards in your language:** Settings → **Display** → **Approval cards in**: English (as Kelvin writes them) or 简体中文 (Simplified Chinese). A card is translated when it is shown, in the portal and on your phone, with its buttons. Names, numbers, units and values stay as Kelvin wrote them, and a line Kelvin can't translate stays in English, whole: a line whose numbers, instrument names or signs wouldn't all come through is shown in English instead. **English** on a card shows the original, which is what you approve; the lab's record keeps English. Each person's own.

- **Copying:** **Copy** under each of Kelvin's answers copies its text; **Copy** under your own message (it shows when you point at the message) copies what you wrote.

- **Each chat's folder:** the folder button under the message box shows where the chat saves its runs; click it to see the whole path, pick a recent folder or choose another. In the Kelvin app, **New chat** asks for a folder with the computer's folder picker. See [Each chat's folder](data.md#each-chats-folder).

- **Models:** Add keys under Settings for Claude, GPT or Gemini, or point it at a local OpenAI-compatible server (Ollama, vLLM, LM Studio) for confidential work. Keys stay in your own settings file (`~/Kelvin/users/<you>/settings.json`, encrypted if you have a password) and go only to their own provider.
  - **Which model a chat starts with:** Settings → Models → **Default model for new chats**. **Automatic**, the default, is Claude Opus: through a Claude API key (`anthropic:claude-opus-5-5`), or else through the Claude Code on this computer (`claudecode:opus`), else the first model there is; the menu says which one it is now. A chat keeps the model it was started or continued with; pick another under the message box for that chat. Claude Sonnet (`claude-sonnet-5-5`), Haiku (`claude-haiku-5-5`) and Fable (`claude-fable-5-1`) are in the list.
  - **A model named without its service** works too, wherever a model is named: `sonnet`, `claude-sonnet-5-5` or `anthropic:sonnet` goes to the Claude API key when there is one, else to Claude Code.
  - **When a service says no,** the chat says why in plain words: a key that is missing, wrong or revoked, or expired; no credit or quota left on the account; a rate limit or your plan's usage limit; the service overloaded or out of reach; Claude Code not signed in, or its sign-in expired; a model the key can't use. It says what to do, and never shows the key.
- **ChatGPT plan:** Instead of an OpenAI API key, you can use your ChatGPT plan. In Settings press **Continue with ChatGPT** and sign in on OpenAI's page in your browser.
  - **What Kelvin gets:** it never sees your password and gets no access to your ChatGPT conversations.
  - **Where it shows:** ChatGPT models then appear as **ChatGPT plan** in the model picker, and **Using ChatGPT plan** shows by the picker while one is selected.
  - **Tokens:** they're kept in `~/Kelvin/users/<you>/chatgpt.json`, renewed automatically, and revoked when you sign out of ChatGPT.
  - **Limits:** use **Manage usage** to set them; project rules can allow or exclude ChatGPT plan by name.
  - **Basis:** this follows OpenAI's Sign in with ChatGPT for open-source and personal local apps.
- **Other model services:** Under Settings → **Other model services**, add any service that speaks the OpenAI Chat Completions API.
  - **Ready-made:** OpenRouter (models from many companies with one key), OpenCode, DeepSeek, Groq, Mistral and xAI. Pick one and paste its key.
  - **Anything else:** choose **Other** and give its address (for example Together, Fireworks, or a LiteLLM gateway).
  - **How each appears:** each service shows under its own name in the model picker, so a cloud service is never counted as a local model. A project's rules can allow or exclude it by name.
  - **Which models:** Kelvin lists the newest models that can call tools (it needs tools), or the ones you name.
  - **OpenCode:** only its Chat Completions models are listed, because its Claude, GPT and Gemini models use other formats.
  - **Security:** keys can also come from environment variables such as `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`. Services on the internet need https.
- **Short answers:** Kelvin keeps answers under 200 words, so you can judge one at a glance. Code and plans don't count.
  - **Condensed:** an answer that still runs longer is condensed by the same model. Every number, unit, run ID, warning and question stays; background and restatements go. **Full answer** shows the whole one, and Kelvin keeps both.
  - **Kept whole:** when you asked for the length (a full derivation, every step, a whole document), the answer stays long, with a note saying why.
  - **Your choice:** Settings → Models → **Answer length**: 100, 200 or 400 words, or any length.
- **Projects:** The sidebar groups conversations into projects, for example one per sample or experiment. Make a project with **+**, start a chat inside it with its **+**, and move a conversation with its **⋯** menu or by dragging it onto a project. Deleting a project keeps its conversations.
- **Memory:** Kelvin learns from conversations, but keeps three levels that never mix:
  - **You, in every chat:** your instructions and the preferences and conventions Kelvin kept, such as units or how careful to be (Settings → Memory).
  - **One project:** that project's instructions and notes, such as decisions, procedures, instrument quirks and results (the project's **⋯ → Project settings**). Only chats in that project see them. Deleting a project deletes its memory.
  - **The conversation itself:** a new chat never sees another chat's history.

  Kelvin keeps short, typed notes, not transcripts, and never questions or chit-chat. It merges a fact it already knows instead of saving it twice. Recalled notes come with your message, and a reply says which ones it used ("Recalled 2 notes"). A large project recalls only the notes that bear on the question. You can delete any note, or switch off **Learn from conversations**.
- **The lab's memory:** beside those three, what everyone here and Kelvin keep about the lab itself, for every chat, every person who signs in on this computer and every agent over MCP ([agents.md](agents.md#the-labs-memory)). A new chat no longer starts from nothing: it knows which channel reads the mixing chamber, that the lock-in takes the chopper's reference, how the sample is excited, and the lessons learned at the bench.
  - **What a note is:** one fact of one kind: a **lesson** (what to do first, what to avoid: "switch each OBIS's emission on before its power"), **wiring** (what is connected where), a **reference** (where a reference comes from), an **excitation** (what drives the sample, and whether it is stated rms or peak) or another **fact** that lasts. A note may name the instruments it concerns.
  - **Who wrote it and when:** every note says so (a person by their account's name, or Kelvin in someone's chat), and keeps what it said before each change.
  - **In the Lab panel:** **Lab memory** lists the notes; **Add…** keeps one, the pencil changes one and the bin deletes one. Anyone at this computer can; phones can't. Every change goes in the lab's record (**Activity → Everyone**).
  - **How chats see it:** every chat's prompt carries a short brief of it (lessons first, then wiring, references, excitations and other facts, within a fixed length), with the measurement chain on record. Kelvin looks up the rest when it needs it. A note tied to an instrument is shown when that instrument is used (when Kelvin reads its card, and the first time a chat sets it or runs on it), and its lessons on the approval cards that name it.
  - **How Kelvin adds to it:** when you tell it such a fact, or a run shows one, Kelvin keeps it and says so in the chat. A chat that has read text from outside the lab since you last wrote (a web page, a paper, a file in its folder) asks you first, since a note reaches every chat. Chats in unpublished, confidential or model-limited projects read the lab's memory but add nothing to it; with **Learn from conversations** off, Kelvin adds nothing either. Incognito chats neither read nor add to it.
  - **Where it is:** `~/Kelvin/lab-memory.json` for your lab (the one in `lab.yaml`) and `~/Kelvin/virtual-lab-memory.json` for the virtual lab, so practising never writes into your lab's memory. A file someone broke by hand is left alone: Kelvin keeps nothing in it until it is fixed.
- **Skills and recipes:** know-how that worked, kept at the same levels (yours for every project, or one project's).
  - **A skill** is a procedure in words: cooling down, aligning a laser through the objective, how this lab finds the Dirac point. Kelvin reads one when a task matches it and follows it unless you say otherwise. Kelvin comes with a few general ones: setting up an instrument, lock-in measurements, cool-downs, finding a resonance, I–V curves, checking a result.
  - **Packed as Claude's skills are:** a folder with `SKILL.md` (its name and when to use it, then the steps) and the files that go with it: a calibration table, a reference figure, a maker's sequence file. Kelvin reads those files when the steps call for them (`read_skill_file`), and never runs a skill's scripts. **Add a skill…** (Settings → **Memory**, or a project's settings) takes a skill made for Claude, as a .zip or .skill file or a lone SKILL.md; **Download** gives one of Kelvin's to use in Claude or another Kelvin. Add only skills from people you trust: a skill tells Kelvin what to do.
  - **Starting one yourself:** type / at the start of a message for the list, and pick one: `/cool-down from 300 K` gives Kelvin that skill's steps with your message, and `/find-gate-peak` runs that recipe.
  - **Switching one off:** each skill and recipe has a switch. Off, Kelvin neither sees nor uses it, and it stays kept. Kelvin's own can be switched off too.
  - **A recipe** is a measurement in stages that checks its own results: each stage a plan, a check on its data (`max(counter.rate) > 2e4`), values kept for the next stage (`at_min(counter.rate, mw.frequency)`), and how often to try again with changed values. Kelvin runs one by its name, every stage through the safety gate like any plan ([data.md](data.md#recipes)).
  - **Keeping them:** when a procedure or a staged measurement worked and will be needed again, Kelvin keeps it and says so in the chat. See, read and delete them in Settings → **Memory** (every project) and in a project's settings. Work in an unshared project keeps what it learned in that project. Incognito chats see only Kelvin's own skills.
- **Confidential projects:** In a project's **Project settings → Who may see this project**, choose which kinds of model may read it (Claude, GPT, Gemini, local models), and whether literature and web search may be used. For confidential work, allow only local models and switch off literature and web search.
  - **Enforcement:** Kelvin refuses any other model for chats, comparisons and second opinions, and leaves out the plan reviewer if it isn't allowed. The composer shows **Confidential**.
  - **The rules stay with the chat:** a chat that ran under them keeps them, even if it is moved out of the project or the project is deleted, so its history never reaches a model you excluded.
- **Kelvin hub:** the hub is one server for Kelvin users in every lab ([hub.md](hub.md)). In Settings → **Account**, under Kelvin hub, type the hub's address and press **Connect**. Kelvin opens the hub in your browser, where you sign in (or register) with your hub password and choose the lab to share into.
  - **What is shared:** your normal projects and unfiled chats, with their whole conversations, and the runs you made in them or outside every project (run.h5 and reports), with the members of that lab. Kelvin sends what changed every two minutes, and what you delete goes from the hub too. **Sync now** sends it at once. If the hub fails on one file (a time-out on a large one, say), the others go on and that file waits: 5 minutes, then twice as long each time, up to 4 hours. Hover over the status line to see what the hub didn't take.
  - **Never shared:** projects marked **Unpublished** or **Confidential** under **Project settings → On the Kelvin hub**, not even their names; projects limited to some models or without literature search; incognito chats and the runs they started; anyone else's runs, even in a data folder you share; API keys; ChatGPT and Claude Code sign-ins. Marking a project takes away what the hub had of it.
  - **Marks stay with chats:** a chat moved out of an unpublished, confidential or model-limited project, or left when the project is deleted, stays unshared. It keeps the limits the project has then, and so does a chat that ran under them, even if they are lifted later. Changing a project's limits doesn't lock chats that didn't run under them. Runs started by a chat that keeps such a mark are never shared, wherever they are filed.
  - **No leaks through notes or samples:** while a chat is in such a project, it keeps no notes for every conversation (those reach shared chats). A run that it starts is filed in its project, without the sample mounted from another project.
  - **Older runs:** runs from before this version of Kelvin, and runs of scans queued before it, don't record who made them or whether an incognito chat started them, so they stay on your computer. Tick **Also share older runs** to share them too, but only if your data folder is yours alone. The status line says how many stay.
  - **One connection each:** everyone on a computer connects as their own Kelvin, so two people (or two accounts of yours) keep their own connections. The hub shows the account name on its connect page. Connecting again, after an update for example, moves what you shared before to the new connection, so the hub keeps one copy.
  - **The token:** Kelvin never sees your hub password. Its token is kept in your settings, encrypted with your password, and never shown in the browser. Kelvin never follows a redirect with it.
  - **Disconnect** stops sharing at once, even in the middle of a sync. What was shared stays on the hub until you delete it on your account page there (**Your account on the hub**).
- **Incognito:** For a question that has nothing to do with your work, press **Incognito** in the top bar; from a saved chat it starts a new incognito one. Kelvin then uses no memory at all (not yours, a project's or the lab's) and keeps none, and the chat is never saved or listed; it is gone when you leave it. The lab's instruments and their limits still apply, and an approval card still shows you the lab's lessons on the instruments it names. Instrument commands and runs are still logged, for safety.
- **Literature:** Kelvin searches arXiv and journal papers (through [OpenAlex](https://openalex.org)) and reads them, so it can plan an experiment from what is known and check a result against published work. Try "what parameters did people use to drive magnons with SAWs in CrSBr? propose a plan".
  - **Search:** results come with authors, venue, citation counts and abstracts.
  - **Reading:** arXiv papers with an HTML version are read in full, section by section, with equations as LaTeX; other papers give their abstract.
  - **Citations:** Kelvin cites what it used as [Author Year](link) and never invents a reference.
  - **Reading list:** in a project, ask Kelvin to keep a paper. It goes on the project's reading list (Project settings), Kelvin sees that list in the project's conversations, and reports list it as references.
  - **Privacy:** only the search words go to arXiv and OpenAlex, never your data or chats. Switch literature and web search off in Settings for confidential work. A free OpenAlex API key (Settings) raises the daily search allowance.
- **The web:** Kelvin searches the web (`web_search`) for what it needs: an instrument's manual or programming guide, its maker's pages, software, drivers and documentation. It reads pages and PDFs (`read_web_page`, with each page's links, documents first) and keeps a manual (`save_manual`). A manual kept for an instrument goes into its folder when Kelvin adds it, and Kelvin writes the instrument's card from it.
  - **Search:** through the web search your model service already offers, so you need no other account. Kelvin uses Claude's web search with a Claude API key, OpenAI's with an OpenAI key, Google Search with a Gemini key, or Claude Code's WebSearch through the Claude Code bridge. It tries the chat's own model first, then any other that can search. With a Claude API key, an admin may need to allow web search in the Claude Console.
  - **Privacy:** only the search words and the pages Kelvin opens leave the computer. The same switch as literature search turns it off, in Settings and per project.
- **Several models at once:** Press **Compare** next to the model picker and tick one or two more models. Each message then goes to all of them, and the answers stream side by side. While comparing, the models can read the lab (status, values, runs, dry runs) but not change it, so three models never drive the same instruments. Press **Continue with this answer** under the one you prefer; the conversation goes on from that answer and with that model.
- **Second opinions:** A different model checks Kelvin's work.
  - **Plans:** pick a reviewer in Settings → **Second opinion on plans**. It then reads every plan before it runs: the request, the lab's state, Kelvin's own checks, and the plan. It answers go, caution or stop with its findings, shown under the plan's step. If it says stop, Kelvin asks you before running the plan, with the findings as reasons.
  - **Answers:** press **Second opinion** under the latest answer and choose a model. It says whether it agrees, partly agrees or disagrees, with its findings, and Kelvin sees them with your next message.
  - **Checked, not trusted:** the reviewer looks up what the answer or the plan rests on, in the lab's records: the runs' data and descriptions, computing from their columns, traces and plots, the instruments' descriptions and manuals, the lab's state and log (up to 10 lookups). Each finding says how serious it is, how sure the reviewer is from what it checked, and the evidence. Only findings it is at least 70% sure of are shown and passed on to Kelvin; the others stay folded under the card. A stop or a disagree needs a sure, serious finding; otherwise it counts as caution or partly.
  - **Re-check:** under an answer's second opinion, **Re-check the findings with another model** has a second model check each finding against the records too. Findings it confirms are marked; findings it can't confirm are set aside.
  - **Limits:** the reviewer only reads: it can't change anything, run a plan or take a camera frame. Its advice doesn't replace the safety gate's limits or approvals. A model from another company gives a more independent view.
- **Pictures:** Kelvin can look at data as a picture, the way you glance at a plot to judge a peak's shape, noise, a jump or where a spot sits (`look_at`): a run's plots as its report draws them, a 2-D scan as a color map, a camera frame from a run or read now. The picture shows under the step. Models that see pictures (Claude, GPT, Gemini, the ChatGPT plan) get it with the caption; others get the caption, which gives the numbers. The latest 8 pictures go along at each step; for values Kelvin uses the numbers, not the pixels.
- **Attachments:** attach photos, screenshots, PDFs or data files with the paperclip, or paste or drop them into the message box: a photo of the wiring, an oscilloscope's screen, a paper behind a paywall, a cycler's export. A data file (CSV, Excel, NumPy, TIFF, BioLogic, Gamry or Arbin) becomes a run as you attach it, and its chip says how many points or frames it has ([Data from other programs](data.md#data-from-other-programs)). Big photos are shrunk to what models read before they are sent. A PDF of up to 20 pages goes whole, figures and all, to a model that reads PDFs (Claude, GPT through an OpenAI key, Gemini); otherwise, and for a longer one, as its text (not its figures: attach a picture of a figure that matters). Either way it is marked for the model as text from outside the lab ([safety.md](safety.md#text-from-outside-the-lab)). A model that can't see pictures (most local models) gets only each picture's name, and Kelvin says so when you send one. Attachments are kept with the chat and go when it is deleted.
- **Files in the chat's folder:** Kelvin lists and reads the files in the folder you chose for the chat (`list_folder`, `read_file`), and nothing outside it: PDFs by pages, text and CSV files, and pictures. A long file is read in parts, and Kelvin reads on where it stopped. A PDF goes to the model as its text, or for the figures, its pages themselves (up to 20 at once) when the model reads PDFs: Claude, GPT through an OpenAI key, and Gemini; other models get the text. What Kelvin reads there is marked as text from outside the lab, and pauses auto mode until you write again ([safety.md](safety.md#text-from-outside-the-lab)). Kelvin also saves text files there (`write_file`), such as a script it wrote for you, notes or a table, without asking; a file it replaces is kept first in `.kelvin-old` beside it. Kelvin's own folder (`~/Kelvin`: lab config, settings, accounts, cards, everyone's chats) is never read or written this way, even from a chat whose folder contains it, and can't be chosen as a chat's folder; nor are the lab's records rewritten ([safety.md](safety.md#the-rules)). Python code in a folder an instrument's library loads from shows in the chat whole, since it runs on the instrument ([safety.md](safety.md#the-rules)).
- **Writing while Kelvin works:** you don't have to wait for its answer. Type and send: the button shows **Stop** while the box is empty. What you send reaches Kelvin after the step it is on (a run or a wait finishes first; **Stop** stops it now) and stands in the chat where Kelvin read it. If Kelvin had already finished, it goes as your next message. A message that only says stop (`STOP`, `stop!`, `stop now`, `abort`, `/stop`) is not passed on: it stops Kelvin's answer and the lab's work at once, as the Stop button does, whoever started it.
- **Several chats at once:** while Kelvin answers in one chat, open another (or a new one) and ask there; up to 4 chats answer side by side. A dot marks a chat that is answering, has a new answer, or (amber) asks for your approval, and a note at the bottom says when an answer or a request comes in a chat you left (click it to go there). Going back to a chat still answering shows its answer as it goes.
  - **Changes go one at a time:** a set, a run, a raw command, starting the queue or adding an instrument is one chat's step at a time, filed under that chat (its runs go to its folder and project). A chat that needs to change the lab while another's step does waits a few seconds (not at all while a longer run, the queue or an approval card holds the lab), then says what holds it, and the model tells you.
  - **Everything else goes on beside it, from any chat:** looking things up (runs and their plots, even the one running; cards, experiments, manuals), reading the instruments, the queue (adding, editing, reordering, pausing; an item is filed under the chat that queued it) and watches. A reading never sends a command to an instrument the run is using, as one in between could upset what the run does there (a lock-in's acquisition, a sweep the instrument takes). Kelvin gives that instrument's latest values instead, with how old each is and whether the run measured or set it, and reads the other instruments as usual.
  - **Stop:** the button stops the chat you are in, and the lab's work too when it is that chat's. Asking Kelvin to stop in any chat (it calls `stop_run`), or typing `STOP`, stops the lab's work whoever started it.
- **Tools when needed:** Kelvin keeps its everyday tools at hand, and loads the rarer ones (setting up instruments and writing cards, installing makers' software, editing the queue, watches, pulse boards, SQL on the catalog) when a chat needs them (`find_tools`), so each step reads less and answers sooner. Lookups that touch no instrument (runs, papers, the web, files) run side by side; changes to the instruments go one at a time, and readings beside them.
- **Waiting:** for a long wait (a cool-down, a temperature to settle, a long queue), Kelvin ends its answer and continues the chat later by itself (`wake_me`): after a while, once a reading holds a condition for some minutes, or when the run or queue ends. Meanwhile you can use Kelvin in other chats. A bar above the message box says when it will continue, with **Don't continue**, and a notification says when it did. A wait inside an answer is at most 2 minutes. A chat is continued at most 12 times a day, only while you are signed in, and never an incognito chat. A wait goes on after Kelvin restarts (an update, say); a wait for a run that the restart stopped ends, and Kelvin looks at how the run ended.
- **Long conversations:** when a conversation grows close to what the model can read at once, Kelvin summarises the earlier part for the model as a lab handoff (what you want, the lab's state, the runs with their IDs and results, decisions, open questions) and keeps the last two exchanges as they are. A note in the chat says so. The chat itself keeps everything, and Kelvin searches it when it needs an earlier detail (`chat_history`).
- **Conversation:** Answers stream in Markdown. Each tool call is a step you can expand, and anything that drives the sample harder appears as an **Approve / Decline** card inside the conversation.
- **Runs pane:** Measurements get their own pane beside the chat, like a terminal pane, with one tab per run. It opens when a plan starts, including plans started by another agent. While the plan runs, the pane shows a progress bar, the points done, the time left and plots that grow point by point, plus a Stop button. When the run ends, Kelvin analyses the data. The conversation gets a short card with the assessment and the next step. The pane shows the key results with uncertainties, buttons for the run's report, slides and data file, `run.h5` (made automatically), the plots with their fits (hover for values), the table and the plan. **Lab notebook**, beside **Download data**, gives the run's record as Markdown with its plots, for an ELN ([data.md](data.md#a-record-for-your-lab-notebook-eln)). Drag the pane's edge to resize it; open earlier runs from its clock button.
- **Lab panel:** Shows the **Sample** mounted now (every scan is filed under it; change it when you change samples), its protection (**Protect this device…**, or **Change…** once it has some: its gate limits, leakage stop and other limits, see [safety.md](safety.md#protecting-the-sample)), **Hands on the sample** (stops what runs and holds everything that reaches the sample at safe until you unlock), live instrument values (drives away from safe are highlighted; while a run goes on, the instruments it uses show their latest values) and the activity log. **Instruments** has **Set up…** (the lab config, scanning, setting up from your lab's own scripts, and **Limits…** for each instrument) and **Cards…** (the instrument cards); see [instruments.md](instruments.md#connecting-instruments). Notices say what waits for you: **Limits not reviewed** (instruments Kelvin added itself, **Review limits…**) and **Cards not reviewed** (instruments on a draft card, **Review cards…**).
  - **On/off switch on each instrument:** switch off the instruments that are not part of this experiment. Kelvin then sends a switched-off instrument nothing at all: no command, no reading, no health check, no alarm watch, no going to safe. Commands and plans that need it are refused and say why. The switch is kept for that lab, for everyone, until someone switches it back on, and the activity log records who switched it. Switching off frees a VISA instrument for other programs (Kelvin closes its session, which sends the instrument nothing); a driver connection is set aside unclosed, because closing some (pulse generators) would send a stop. Not while a plan or the queue runs.
- **Help:** **Help** at the bottom of the sidebar searches this documentation (the same pages Kelvin reads with `kelvin_help`) and lists its topics to open. You can also ask Kelvin where something is or how a rule works: its instructions carry a map of this portal, so it names the actual buttons instead of guessing.
- **Getting started:** a checklist on the start screen, and in **Help**, says what a lab needs before you trust Kelvin with it, each with **Do it** to go there: **your lab is set up** (your own instruments, not the virtual lab), **instruments checked** (each connected, on a card a person reviewed), **limits set for your setup** (every output with the lab's own limits, not only its card's, the maker's full range; and those of instruments Kelvin added reviewed), **the lab owner's PIN**, **a phone paired** (so a request or an alarm in the night reaches you) and **the data folder**. It reads what Kelvin keeps and asks no instrument anything. **Hide** takes it off the start screen; Help keeps it.
- **Safety:** Kelvin uses the same MCP tools and safety gate as external agents, so it has the same rules and no extra power. Only one conversation changes the lab at a time (the others read, look things up and use the queue meanwhile); Stop ends the current answer and any ramp.

## Calibration

Kelvin keeps each instrument's calibration as you record it: **Lab** → **Set up…** → **Calibration…** on the instrument's row (instruments on this computer, not simulated ones).
- **What you record:** when it was calibrated, when it is due again, the certificate's number, who calibrated it, and the certificate itself (a PDF, or a photo of it), kept in the instrument's folder (`~/Kelvin/instruments/<name>/calibration.json` and `certificates/`). The serial number is the one the instrument gives in its identity reply. Every record is kept, so a run's record says which calibration was in force when it ran.
- **Its row** says where it stands: no calibration on record, calibrated and due on a date, due within 30 days, or **out of date** since a date.
- **When one is out of date,** `health_check` says so for that instrument, and so does every approval card that names it (*Calibration out of date: dmm was due 2026-09-30 (certificate C-1234)*), kept with the card in the lab record. It is a warning, not a stop: the instrument works, and its readings say what they say. `health_check` also says when one is due within 30 days, and a card when the instrument now reports another serial number than the calibration's (another unit at that address).
- **Kelvin** reads it (`instrument_info` says the calibration on record) but never records one: that is a person's, and in the lab record with their name.

## Terminal

The **Terminal** button in the top bar (or Ctrl+`) opens a console at the foot of the window. There you can type to Kelvin, or send an instrument its own commands.
- **Talking to Kelvin:** type as in a chat. Kelvin's steps show as `●` lines with their results below, a run's progress counts up, and its data appear live in the Runs pane. The conversation is saved like any other chat.
- **An instrument's own commands:** `/use lockin` (or just `/lockin`) sends what you type to that instrument, such as `*IDN?`, `SENS?` or `:SOUR:VOLT 1`. Queries show the reply; anything else shows what it did. `/kelvin` goes back to talking to Kelvin.
- **The same safety gate:** limits, interlocks and blocked commands apply as for Kelvin. A change that drives the sample harder asks **approve? [y/N]** right in the terminal. Moving back toward safe doesn't ask. A terminal command never lands in the middle of a run: while Kelvin is busy it is refused, not queued. Every command is in the lab record as yours (`human (terminal)`), and Kelvin's **undo** never undoes it.
- **Other commands:**
  - `/ls`: the instruments.
  - `/status [name]`: readings, outputs and settings.
  - `/get X`, `/set time_constant 300 ms`, `/set smu.V 0.5`: read or set by name.
  - `/describe [name]`: units, limits and safe values.
  - `/manual <words>`: search the instrument's manual.
  - `/new`: start a new conversation with Kelvin.
  - `/stop`: stop Kelvin's answer, any ramp or run, and the queue.
  - `/clear`: clear the screen.
- **Keys:** Up and Down recall what you typed. Tab completes commands, instrument names and parameters. Esc stops Kelvin's answer.

## Updates and one Kelvin per computer

Kelvin keeps itself up to date. Twice a day it looks for a newer version, downloads it, checks it against the checksum GitHub gives for the file, and installs it when you close Kelvin. **Restart to update** in the bar at the top installs it at once. It never installs while a plan, the queue or a chat is running. Settings → **Updates** chooses Automatic, Ask me first, or Off for everyone on the computer, and has **Check now**. On macOS Kelvin says when a new version is out and links to it.

There is only ever one Kelvin on a computer: an update or a new installer replaces the installed one in place, keeping your settings, chats and data. If you start another copy (say, one unzipped somewhere), Kelvin says where the installed one is, so you can delete the extra copy.

## Phone and remote access

Check on a run from the coffee room, or answer an approval from home. Phone access is **off** until you switch it on in Settings → **Phone access**. When it's on, Kelvin also listens on the network (port 8771 by default). Your desktop's access key never works there; only paired phones do.

1. Switch on **Phone access**. Kelvin shows its addresses: on this network, and on Tailscale if you use it.
2. Name the phone, choose what it may do, and press **Pair a phone**.
   - **Watch:** see the lab, runs and alarms, stop a run, and decline requests.
   - **Control:** also chat with Kelvin and approve requests.
3. On the phone, scan the QR code, or open the address and type the 6-digit code. The code works once, for 10 minutes, and repeated wrong codes lock pairing for a while.

**If the phone can't reach Kelvin:** it must be on the same Wi-Fi, and the computer's firewall must let it in. On Windows, press **Let phones through** in Settings → Phone: Windows asks you to allow it. Many university, housing and hotel Wi-Fi networks keep devices apart; on those, install Tailscale on both and pair again. The QR code holds every address Kelvin has, and the iPhone app tries each.

The phone then opens Kelvin's phone page: alarms with **Got it**, requests with **Approve** (control) or **Decline**, the run in progress with its progress and **Stop**, the instruments, and recent notices. A control phone can also open the full portal.

**Kelvin for iPhone** does the same as a native app, and more. With [Kelvin accounts](#kelvin-accounts-one-account-every-computer), sign in to it with your Kelvin account: it finds every Kelvin where you are signed in, through the Kelvin hub, with no pairing and from any network, and goes directly when it can. Without an account, pair it by scanning the same QR code, or by typing the address and code. It can be paired with several Kelvin computers, keeps each one's token in the iPhone's Keychain, and tries the address that worked last first and then the others, so it moves between the lab network and Tailscale by itself. Its tabs:

- **Lab:** alarms, requests waiting for you, the run in progress with a live plot and **Stop**, the instruments (drives not at their safe value in orange), recent notices.
- **Chat** (control phones): your conversations, a new or incognito chat, the answer as it arrives with Kelvin's steps, and requests to approve right there. Tap the microphone to talk instead of typing: the iPhone turns your words into text itself, the audio never leaves it, and you check the message before sending.
- **Runs:** recent runs with their plots, and their report and data to open on the phone.
- **Computers:** your Kelvin account and its Kelvins, switch between computers, pair another, unpair, sign out.

Without a lab computer at hand, **Try a demo lab** shows a made-up one inside the app. The app tells you about new requests and alarms while it is open. For alerts while it is closed, add an email account or an ntfy topic under Notifications. A request nobody answers at the computer comes to the phone, and waits there for you (Settings → Notifications). Building the app: [ios.md](ios.md).

Settings, instruments, the lab config, the approval mode and pairing work only on the Kelvin computer. Remove a phone in Settings, or with **Forget this phone** on the phone itself.

Each person pairs their own phones, and a phone works while its person is signed in on the Kelvin computer: it sees that person's chats and runs, never someone else's.

**Away from the lab:** install [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com) (a free, encrypted VPN) on the lab computer and on the phone, and pair the phone using the Tailscale address. On a plain Wi-Fi network traffic isn't encrypted, so on a shared network prefer Tailscale. Don't forward Kelvin's port to the internet. For push notifications to the phone, add an ntfy topic under Notifications.
