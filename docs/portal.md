# Using Kelvin

The portal is where you chat with Kelvin: the desktop app's window, or the site that `kelvin` opens in your browser.

## Signing in

Everyone who uses Kelvin on a lab computer has an account, and everyone is equal: there is no administrator. A lab can set an owner's PIN that guards limits, auto mode and unlocking Hands on the sample ([safety.md](safety.md#the-lab-owners-pin)), and on top of it give people roles: owner, operator and trainee ([safety.md](safety.md#lab-roles)). Anyone at the computer can add themselves with **Add a person** on the sign-in screen; with roles, a new account is a trainee until the owner gives it a role.

- **Yours alone:** your settings and API keys, ChatGPT sign-in, Kelvin hub connection, chats, projects and memory, your data folder (runs and reports), your queue, the sample you mounted, and your record (`events.jsonl`).
- **Everyone's:** the instruments, the lab config, the instrument cards, the lab's memory, the switches for setting up instruments and phone access, and the lab's **instrument log** (`~/Kelvin/instrument-log.jsonl`): every command and approval from everyone, with the name of whoever was signed in. The Lab panel's **Activity**, set to **Everyone**, shows it.
- **Passwords are optional.** With one, your API keys, ChatGPT sign-in and Kelvin hub token are encrypted with it. Nobody can reset a forgotten password, because there is no administrator; your runs and reports are still in your data folder. Change or remove it in Settings → **Your account**.
- **One person, no password:** Kelvin signs you in by itself when it starts, so a single user never sees the sign-in screen.
- **Signing in while the instruments are set up:** Kelvin opens at once and connects the lab's instruments in the background, which can take minutes (a slow instrument, a maker's driver, reading every setting). You can sign in meanwhile: the sign-in screen says what the setup is doing. Then **Setting up your instruments** shows each step (which instrument it is connecting, then reading their settings) until they are ready, and closes by itself. **Use Kelvin meanwhile** closes it; Kelvin uses each instrument once it is connected.
- **Signing out** (bottom of the sidebar) declines open approvals and turns the approval mode back to asking: one person's yes is never another's. A run or queue you leave running goes on, and is recorded as yours. Nobody else can sign in until it ends; anyone can press **Stop it** on the sign-in screen.
- **Lab roles and the lab config's history** (Settings → **Instruments**): the owner, signed in, gives each person a role with **Give roles…** and the PIN, and with **Where my notifications go** has every safety change sent where their own notifications go, whoever is signed in. **Lab config history…** (also **History…** in Lab setup) lists every version of the lab config and the lab's cards, and each change to who may do what, with who, when and why; **Diff** shows what changed, **Roll back to this…** puts a version back. With roles, a trainee who approves a request is asked who confirms it: an operator or the owner picks their name and types their password, or the owner types the PIN ([safety.md](safety.md#lab-roles)).
- **Before accounts:** the first account takes over what Kelvin kept until then: chats and runs in `~/Kelvin/virtual-lab`, and the API keys.
- **What accounts don't do:** they keep people's work apart inside Kelvin, but they don't lock files. Anyone who can open this computer's files can read the folders.
- **Removing someone from this computer:** before anyone signs in, **Remove someone from this computer** on the sign-in screen lists every account here (old ones from before Kelvin accounts too); signed in, Settings → **Account** → **Delete this account…** does the same for you. It takes that person's password (none for an account without one). It removes what uninstalling Kelvin would remove for them: settings and API keys, the ChatGPT sign-in (revoked with OpenAI first), chats, projects and memory. Their measurements stay: runs, reports and record, in their data folder. A new account never gets their folder, nor what Kelvin kept before accounts if they had taken it over. Refused while a plan or the queue runs.

Each person's things are in `~/Kelvin/users/<name>/`: `settings.json`, `chatgpt.json`, `hub-sync.json` (what was shared on the Kelvin hub), `chats/` and `data/` (their default data folder; Settings → **Data folder** chooses another).

### Kelvin accounts: one account, every computer

People sign in with a **Kelvin account**: email and password, the same on every computer in every lab (`central.py`). There is one [Kelvin hub](hub.md) for every Kelvin user, built into Kelvin (`central.DEFAULT_HUB`: `kelvin.tail269ee5.ts.net` for now, until it moves to its permanent server). It keeps only the accounts. Chats, data and API keys stay on each computer, as above.

- **Create a Kelvin account** on the sign-in screen: name, email, a password of 8 characters or more, and agreeing to the hub's terms and privacy notice. The hub mails a link to confirm the address.
- **First sign-in on a computer:** Kelvin asks the hub. If the computer has accounts from before, Kelvin asks whether one of them is yours. That one (with its password on this computer, if it had one) then keeps its chats, data and API keys. **None of these: start fresh** makes a new folder.
- **Offline:** after one sign-in on a computer, Kelvin keeps a hash of the password there, so the same password signs you in while the hub can't be reached. Wrong passwords someone tries for your email elsewhere don't hold off your sign-in on a computer you use: the hub counts them per computer.
- **Changing your password:** Settings → **Account**, while Kelvin reaches the hub. It changes on the hub, and your keys here are encrypted again with the new one. On another computer, the new password works the next time it reaches the hub. Your keys there were encrypted with the old one, so Kelvin asks for it once (**Keep my keys**). **Skip** leaves them unreadable; add them again in Settings.
- **Forgot your password?** On the sign-in screen: the hub mails a link to choose a new one. The old one still opens a computer that can't reach the hub, until it does.
- **Name, email confirmation, export:** on your account page on the hub (Settings → **Account** → **Your account on the Kelvin hub**).
- **Deleting your Kelvin account:** tick **Also delete my Kelvin account, on every computer** when you remove it (above), or use **Delete account** on your account page on the hub. The hub checks the password and forgets the account; this computer then removes it too. Other computers keep your account there, unable to sign in, until it is removed there as well. Without the hub, remove it from this computer only.
- **One hub for everyone:** there is nothing to set up per lab. The sign-in screen and Settings → **Account** show the hub. `account_hub` in `~/Kelvin/settings.json` names another, only for development; left empty, it is Kelvin's own again. Changing it (**change** beside the hub) takes the lab owner's PIN, or with no PIN set, nobody else's account on this computer, so nobody can point the others' sign-in at a hub of their own.
- **Your phone:** sign in to Kelvin for iPhone with the same Kelvin account. It reaches every Kelvin where you are signed in: directly on the lab network, and through the hub from anywhere else ([Phone and remote access](#phone-and-remote-access)).
- **For now** Kelvin shares no work with the hub: Settings → **Account** has no hub connection, and projects have no hub setting (`HUB_SHARING` in `web/app.py`).

## In the portal

- **Welcome:** on a computer whose lab has no instruments yet, Kelvin opens on **Welcome to Kelvin**: three steps with a title and a control on each row, and nothing to read. **Workspace**: **Scripts** (the folders of the lab's own scripts: **Add folder…** adds one (the computer's folder picker in the Kelvin app, Kelvin's folder browser in a browser), **Remove** on its row takes it off; Kelvin reads them for the instruments and values they use, never runs them), **Data** (where runs, the lab record and reports go; **Change…** chooses another the same way) and **Import** (the scripts to take into the sequence's functions, all ticked, every script listed; a long list scrolls in the card). **Model**: an API key for **Claude**, **OpenAI** or **Gemini**, **ChatGPT** (**Continue** signs in with your ChatGPT plan), **Claude Code** (when it is on this computer) or **Local** (a model server's address). **Instruments**: **Scan**, then **Add** the ones found. **Next** does what its step asks, **Done** what is left on every step; **Back** (from the second step) and the dots go back to a step already seen without doing anything; **Skip** (top right) ends it. On a tall window the card stands in the middle of it; the head, the dots and **Skip** stay in one place from step to step. It opens Home at the end, and doesn't open again in that browser. Everything it sets is in Settings and **Lab** → **Set up…** afterwards.

- **The top bar:** it runs across the whole window, above the conversation list, the chat and the panels, and nothing in it moves when the list hides, a panel opens or a panel goes full screen. At its left: ≡ (the conversation list), Kelvin's mark, then the chat's title. In the middle: **Chat** | **Home**, centred on the window. At the right: the panels' buttons, each named by its panel (**Terminal**, **Team chat**, **Analysis**, **Runs**, **Lab**); pointing at one shows the name. On a narrow window the chat's title goes first, then the mark. On a phone (560 px or less) the bar takes two rows, with **Chat** | **Home** on the second; under about 430 px the chat's title has no room there and is left out.

- **The notice row:** one row under the top bar holds what you must see or answer: the newest alarm nobody has said **Got it** to (with its **Got it**; the next one shows when you have), **Hands on the sample** with the only **Unlock…**, and the update bar (**Restart to update**). When more alarms wait, one row under the newest counts them ("11 more alarms"); pressing it opens **Lab** → **Watches** with **Alarm history** open, where every alarm is listed. So the row never grows past a few lines, however many alarms come. It shows in every layout, a full-screen panel included, and several notices stand side by side while they fit.
- **Short messages:** a message for a moment (a chat that answered while you were elsewhere, a refusal) stands just above the message box, centred on the chat; on Home, or with a panel alone on the screen, it stands near the bottom of what shows there. It never lies over the message box's buttons. One that names a chat opens it when clicked.
- **Focus:** a button, list, switch or menu item you press with the mouse or a finger shows no ring around it, nor does the message box. The keyboard (Tab, the arrows, Esc) shows where it is with an outline, at once. A text field you type in shows it by its border.

- **Questions:** Kelvin asks in a small dialog in the page, never in the browser's own box. Its first line is the question and the reason is under it; the buttons name what they do (**Delete** and **Cancel**, say). Esc, **Cancel**, or a click on the dim page around it mean no. A click in the first moment, or the second click of a double-click, is not taken as an answer, and when the answer can't be undone, **Cancel** is the button chosen first. No dialog is taller than the window: its title and its buttons stay where they are, and what is between them scrolls.

- **Chat | Home:** the switch in the middle of the top bar, as ChatGPT's Chat | Work. **Home** takes the chat's place and fits the window without scrolling. At the top is one line: a greeting, what the lab does now (an alarm, an approval waiting with **Open**, the block running and how far the sequence is, or ready) and the lab's name. Below it are three tiles on one row: **Sequence** (how far it is, and what runs or comes next), **Runs** (today's runs and the latest ones) and **Lab** (the instruments on, alarms, and the readings you watch, with a line of the first one over the last ten minutes). Each tile is a link to its panel: it shows the panel, or rings the panel's head when it is already on the screen, and never closes it. While an alarm is on, **Lab** opens on **Watches**, where the alarm is; otherwise on the part you chose last. The **Sequence** tile's state reads as the Sequence's bar does: **Running** while your own **Pause** waits for the running block, and the reason of any pause as its tip. Kelvin's blocks fill the rest of the height, pinned ones first, then the newest; when there are more than fit, they scroll on their own. On a big screen (Home at least 1040 px wide and 1200 tall, as on a 1440p monitor; a 1080p screen, a laptop or a tablet keeps the one row) it is drawn larger: the greeting on a line of its own over what the lab does now, **Sequence** and **Runs** side by side with **Lab** under them across the width, and the blocks three to a row in a centred column. When panels beside Home leave it narrow, or it is short (under 720 px: a small window, or the terminal or the notices under the top bar taking its height), each tile becomes one line: its name, its number and its state. Kelvin puts a block there when a run gives a result worth keeping (its number with the error and unit, and the run), when it has a next step for you (with a button: **Add to sequence** adds its scan, or one that asks Kelvin about it), a reading to watch live, a note or a checklist; it keeps a block up to date rather than adding another, and takes off what no longer holds. Pin a block to keep it first, tick a checklist, or take a block off with ×. A block shows how long ago it came after its title when that fits on the title's last line, and three lines of its text (two on a laptop); point at the text to read all of it. **Pin** and **×** are always there, faintly. Home is yours, in your data folder (`home.json`, beside the queue), so it follows you when you sign in; an incognito chat never puts anything there. **Chat** brings the conversation back; opening or starting a chat does too. Kelvin always opens on the chat, whichever side was on when you left it.

- **Sequence:** the second tab of the **Runs** pane (**Scan** | **Sequence** at its top; the pane opens on the one you showed last in that browser) shows the queue as blocks, and only the queue: **＋ Add** under the blocks goes to the **Scan**, where the scans, scripts and experiments are; a step (**Set** | **Wait** | **Settle**) and **Paste a plan** are under the blocks. Each block is two lines in its kind's colour (a scan, a step or a wait, one of your scripts, a lab experiment): its number, name and state, then what it does (a failed block: why it failed) and its tools, always faintly shown. A running block fills with its colour from the left as it goes (its front edge blinks softly), with "12/40 · 4:58 left" on its second line; a done block's name is dimmed, and a skipped block is faded. The bar above the blocks is one line: the sequence's name (click to rename it), its state, **Start**, **Pause**, **Stop** and **⋯**. **Start** asks the queue's one approval as ever; it reads **Resume** after a pause, and **Retry** after a pause its failed block made (that block again, then on; a pause for anything else reads **Resume**, even when an earlier block failed and the sequence went on). When there is nothing to start it is greyed, and its tip says why. **Pause** stops after the running block: until then it reads **Pausing…** in amber, and a second press takes your pause back, so the sequence goes on (a press from a screen that was out of date is refused, and the bar says why). A pause that Kelvin, a failure, a guard, a block's **Pause after** or **Stop** made has no Pause button and can't be taken back: the state reads Pausing, with the reason as its tip, and once the sequence has paused, **Resume** or **Retry** goes on. **Stop** ends the running block now (its drives go to safe) and pauses. In a narrow pane Pause and Stop show their icons only, with their names as tips; **Pausing…** keeps its word. **⋯**: **On a failure**, a choice of **Pause** or **Go on** (a dot on the chosen one), **Sweep…** (the ticked blocks again for each value of one setting), **Save as…**, **Load** (adds a saved sequence at the end), **Clear finished**, and **Kelvin replies at once** (below), a box ticked or empty.
  - **A block:** drag it to move it (or Alt+↑/↓); ⧉ copies it below itself; ↺ puts a finished one back in line; ⏭ skips it (the running one ends now and the sequence goes on); × removes it. A click opens its settings in place: a script's values (as its file has them until you change one), a step's wait and set values, a scan's **Edit scan** (the scan editor); **Pause after**, a switch, pauses the sequence after it; it acts at once, and refused, it turns back and says why under itself.
  - **Functions:** what can go into the sequence: your scripts, the lab's experiments and every scan Kelvin ran (your library), one line each (the name, and its file when there is room; the whole path in its tip), with **⋯** on each. From a pane 600 px wide they are a column beside the blocks (two columns of them in full screen). In a narrower pane, **＋ Add** under the blocks opens them below the blocks, each part scrolling on its own (a drag from one to the other works), and a second press closes them. A click adds one at the end, a drag puts it where you drop it. Under them, **Step**, one line until you use it (a click on a kind opens its fields), types one: **Set** a parameter to a value (the lab's settings are offered, with their units), **Wait** a time, or **Settle**: wait until a reading settles (within, for, a timeout); **Add** puts it at the end, checked as any item is. **Paste a plan** takes any item as written. Each has rename, a colour, **Edit** (a scan) or **Versions** (an experiment), and remove. **＋ New function** takes a Python script or notebook: pick it from the lab's scripts folders (each folder's under its name when there are several) or give its path, see the settings Kelvin found in it (its module's constants and main()'s defaults), give it a name and a colour. A LabVIEW VI chosen there is translated into one of the lab's experiments at once, under the name you give it (an experiment has no colour); while it goes on it is listed among the experiments, and then its draft, with **Review** and × to let it go (see **One VI on its own** under **From your LabVIEW…**). **New scan** opens the scan editor.
  - **Kelvin hears what you do:** each change you make to the sequence (start, pause, stop, skip, remove, move, change a block's settings, load, clear) and on Home (a block taken off, ticked or pinned, a suggestion added to the sequence) is told to the chat it concerns, in one line in that chat: the chat that queued the block, started the sequence or put the block on Home. A chat answering then reads it at its next step; any other reads it with your next message there. A block you added yourself concerns no chat: every chat of yours reads such changes with its next message. A block that fails is told the same way. Nothing calls a model for this unless you switch on **Kelvin replies at once**: then a stop or a failure continues the chat it concerns at once (at most 12 times a day per chat, as any wake-up).

- **Settings:** (at the bottom of the conversation list) a list of sections at the left, one section at a time at the right. What acts at once stands in a card of its own at the top of its section: **Compare models** on **Models**, **Page size** on **Display**, **Phone access** on **Phone**, and, signed in with a Kelvin account, the switch that lets your phone reach this Kelvin through the hub on **Account**. On these pages a switch acts at once and a tick box waits for **Save**, as every box and list does (a key's **Remove** too: the key goes on **Save**; greyed while nothing is set there, a key, the webhook's URL or the mail's password). In a row, a skill's switch on **Memory** (refused, it turns back and says why under the skill) and a service's **Pictures** on **Models** (**Auto**, **On** or **Off**) act at once too. Any other button acts when you press it (**Add folder…**, **Connect**, **Use this folder**, **Check now**; the roles that **Give roles…** shows are kept by **Save roles**). While nothing waits, **Save** is greyed and the button beside it reads **Close**. Once a change waits, **Save** can be pressed, the button beside it reads **Cancel**, and a dot after a section's name in the list shows where the change is. **Cancel** lets go of only what waits for Save, never of what acted at once; the next time Settings opens, every field shows what is saved. × and Esc do what **Close** does, but with a change waiting they ask first whether to discard your changes on the sections with a dot (**Discard**, or **Keep editing** to stay). While **Save** is on its way, nothing closes Settings: its answer does. Enter in a box never closes Settings: it presses the button on the box's own row (**Connect**, **Add**, **Change**, **Use this folder**) and otherwise does nothing; in **Current password**, or a service's name or address, it goes on to the next box, and in an empty **New password** it does nothing. On a local account, **Change** with an empty new password takes your password off, after asking (**Remove your password?**). If **Save** is refused, Settings stays open, says why at the bottom and keeps your changes.

- **Page size:** Settings → **Display** → **Page size** makes the whole page smaller or bigger, from 80% to 150%, in a browser and in the Kelvin app alike. Left on **Auto**, Kelvin picks it for the window: 100% on a big window (1600 × 900 or more), on a touch screen and on a window under 1000 px wide; 85% on a window under 680 px tall; 90% otherwise. − and + choose a size; **Auto** goes back. It is kept on this browser, since the monitor and the laptop differ. In the Kelvin app, Ctrl + and Ctrl −, or Ctrl and the mouse wheel, step it too, and Ctrl 0 goes back to Auto; in a browser those keys stay the browser's own zoom, which Auto follows. A Text size set in the app before carries over once.
  - **Fitting the window:** the page never scrolls as a whole. Each view fits the window, and only its lists scroll inside their place (the chat, Home's blocks, the sequence, the Scan's functions, a run, the Lab panel's parts, a dialog's middle, a Settings page), with a soft edge where more waits. On a shorter window the spacing tightens and minor lines go first, before anything has to scroll.

- **Approval cards in your language:** Settings → **Display** → **Approval cards in**: English (as Kelvin writes them) or 简体中文 (Simplified Chinese). A card is translated when it is shown, in the portal and on your phone, with its buttons. Names, numbers, units and values stay as Kelvin wrote them, and a line Kelvin can't translate stays in English, whole: a line whose numbers, instrument names or signs wouldn't all come through is shown in English instead. A translated card has a choice of two beside its title, **中文** | **English**: **English** shows the original, which is what you approve; the lab's record keeps English. Each person's own.

- **Copying:** the copy icon under each of Kelvin's answers copies its text; the one under your own message (it shows when you point at the message) copies what you wrote. Pointing at it says **Copy**, and it turns into a tick once copied. Answers are headed **Kelvin**, whichever model Kelvin uses; the message box shows the model.

- **Each chat's folder:** the folder button under the message box shows where the chat saves its runs; click it to see the whole path, pick the data folder or a recent folder (a dot marks the one in use) or choose another. **New chat** asks for a folder first. See [Each chat's folder](data.md#each-chats-folder).

- **Choosing a folder:** the Kelvin app opens the computer's own folder picker. In a browser on the Kelvin computer, which can't give a folder's path itself, Kelvin's folder browser opens instead, titled by what you are choosing: the path at the top (type or paste one; Enter goes there), **Home** and the drives, **Up** and the folders on the way, then the folders in it. A click or Enter goes into one, ↑ ↓ move along them, Backspace goes up and Esc cancels. It shows how many scripts (.py and .ipynb) the folder holds, so you see it is the right one; **Choose this folder** picks it. For a chat's folder or the data folder, a path you typed that isn't there yet is taken with **Use it anyway**, and the folder is made then. A network folder that doesn't answer within a few seconds is said so. It lists folder names only, never what is in a file, and leaves out hidden and system folders; only a browser on the Kelvin computer can use it, never a phone.

- **Models:** signed in with your Kelvin account, the hub's models are in the picker: Claude, GPT, Gemini, OpenCode Zen and OpenCode Go through the Kelvin hub, with the hub owner's keys and a monthly budget, which Settings → **Models** shows ([hub.md](hub.md#models-through-the-hub)). Confidential projects never use them; point Kelvin at a local OpenAI-compatible server (Ollama, vLLM, LM Studio) for confidential work. The Kelvin app takes no API keys of its own; only Kelvin from source, for its developers, also takes keys for Claude, GPT or Gemini, kept in your own settings file (`~/Kelvin/users/<you>/settings.json`, encrypted if you have a password) and sent only to their own provider.
  - **Which model a chat starts with:** Settings → Models → **Default model for new chats**. **Automatic**, the default, is Claude Opus: through a Claude API key (`anthropic:claude-opus-5-5`), or else through the Claude Code on this computer (`claudecode:opus`), else the first model there is; the menu says which one it is now. A chat keeps the model it was started or continued with; pick another under the message box for that chat. Claude Sonnet (`claude-sonnet-5-5`), Haiku (`claude-haiku-5-5`) and Fable (`claude-fable-5-1`) are in the list.
  - **A model named without its service** works too, wherever a model is named: `sonnet`, `claude-sonnet-5-5` or `anthropic:sonnet` goes to the Claude API key when there is one, else to Claude Code.
  - **When a service says no,** the chat says why in plain words: a key that is missing, wrong or revoked, or expired; no credit or quota left on the account; a rate limit or your plan's usage limit; the service overloaded or out of reach; Claude Code not signed in, or its sign-in expired; a model the key can't use. It says what to do, and never shows the key. Anything else is the service's own words after its name, on one line ("OpenCode Go: Model is unavailable"), or, when it gave none, its error's number ("OpenCode Go refused the request (error 400)."); a second opinion, a card's draft, a VI's translation or a project's opening that a service turns down says it the same way.
- **ChatGPT plan:** Instead of an OpenAI API key, you can use your ChatGPT plan. In Settings press **Continue with ChatGPT** and sign in on OpenAI's page in your browser.
  - **What Kelvin gets:** it never sees your password and gets no access to your ChatGPT conversations.
  - **Where it shows:** ChatGPT models then appear as **ChatGPT plan** in the model picker, and **Using ChatGPT plan** shows by the picker while one is selected.
  - **Tokens:** they're kept in `~/Kelvin/users/<you>/chatgpt.json`, renewed automatically, and revoked when you sign out of ChatGPT.
  - **Limits:** use **Manage usage** to set them; project rules can allow or exclude ChatGPT plan by name.
  - **Basis:** this follows OpenAI's Sign in with ChatGPT for open-source and personal local apps.
- **Other model services:** Under Settings → **Models** → **Other model services** (it folds under its title), add any service that speaks the OpenAI Chat Completions API.
  - **Ready-made:** OpenRouter (models from many companies with one key), OpenCode, DeepSeek, Groq, Mistral and xAI. Pick one and paste its key.
  - **Anything else:** choose **Other** and give its address (for example Together, Fireworks, or a LiteLLM gateway).
  - **How each appears:** each service shows under its own name in the model picker, so a cloud service is never counted as a local model. A project's rules can allow or exclude it by name.
  - **Pictures:** each service has a **Pictures** choice under its address, which acts at once. At first Kelvin finds out model by model which of its models see pictures (below); **On** or **Off** says it for the whole service, for a gateway or a model on your own computer that Kelvin can't tell about.
  - **Which models:** Kelvin lists the newest models that can call tools (it needs tools), or the ones you name.
  - **Names it doesn't offer:** when Kelvin can read the service's own list, it checks the models you name against it, when you add the service and on **Test**, and says which the service doesn't offer and the nearest it does ("OpenCode Go doesn't offer deepseek-flash (nearest: deepseek-v4.1-flash)."). A name it lists with `:latest` (Ollama's `llama3.3`) or without the variant after the last colon (OpenRouter's `:online`, `:nitro` or `:floor`) is offered. A service that turns the key down when asked for its list says so there too, so **Test** says **Problem**, not **Works**. The service is added all the same, with the names as you typed them: a service may serve a name it doesn't list.
  - **OpenCode:** only its Chat Completions models are listed, because its Claude, GPT and Gemini models use other formats.
  - **OpenCode's sessions:** OpenCode routes and caches by conversation, and OpenCode Go refuses a request without `x-opencode-session`. Your own OpenCode and OpenCode Go (or a service at OpenCode's address under another name) send one with every request, as the Kelvin hub's do: each chat its own (a hash of it, never the chat's id), in its answers and the summary of a long chat, and one of the service's own for what Kelvin asks outside a chat (a project's opening, a card, the list of models).
  - **Security:** keys can also come from environment variables such as `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`. Services on the internet need https.
- **Short answers:** Kelvin asks the model to keep answers under 200 words, so you can judge one at a glance. Code and plans don't count.
  - **How:** the budget is in the model's instructions and comes again with each message you send, so the model writes a short answer itself. Kelvin doesn't rewrite it.
  - **Longer when asked:** ask for a full derivation, every step or a whole document, and the answer is as long as it needs to be.
  - **Your choice:** Settings → Models → **Answer length**: 100, 200 or 400 words, or any length.
- **Projects:** The sidebar groups conversations into projects, for example one per sample or experiment. Make a project with **+**, start a chat inside it with its **+**, and move a conversation with its **⋯** menu (a dot marks its project; **No project** takes it out) or by dragging it onto a project. A click on a project's name folds its conversations away or shows them again, the project of the open chat too (its name then looks chosen); the browser remembers which are folded. Deleting a project keeps its conversations. At the bottom of the sidebar, the lab's name is only a label: the **Lab** button in the top bar opens the Lab panel.
- **Memory:** Kelvin learns from conversations, but keeps three levels that never mix:
  - **You, in every chat:** your instructions and the preferences and conventions Kelvin kept, such as units or how careful to be (Settings → Memory).
  - **One project:** that project's instructions and notes, such as decisions, procedures, instrument quirks and results (the project's **⋯ → Project settings**). Only chats in that project see them. Deleting a project deletes its memory.
  - **The conversation itself:** a new chat never sees another chat's history.

  Kelvin keeps short, typed notes, not transcripts, and never questions or chit-chat. It merges a fact it already knows instead of saving it twice. Recalled notes come with your message, and a reply says which ones it used ("Recalled 2 notes"). A large project recalls only the notes that bear on the question. You can delete any note, or switch off **Learn from conversations**.
- **The lab's memory:** beside those three, what everyone here and Kelvin keep about the lab itself, for every chat, every person who signs in on this computer and every agent over MCP ([agents.md](agents.md#the-labs-memory)). A new chat no longer starts from nothing: it knows which channel reads the mixing chamber, that the lock-in takes the chopper's reference, how the sample is excited, and the lessons learned at the bench.
  - **What a note is:** one fact of one kind: a **lesson** (what to do first, what to avoid: "switch each OBIS's emission on before its power"), **wiring** (what is connected where), a **reference** (where a reference comes from), an **excitation** (what drives the sample, and whether it is stated rms or peak) or another **fact** that lasts. A note may name the instruments it concerns.
  - **Who wrote it and when:** every note says so (a person by their account's name, or Kelvin in someone's chat), and keeps what it said before each change.
  - **In the Lab panel:** **Memory** lists the notes; **Add…** keeps one, the pencil changes one and the bin deletes one. Anyone at this computer can; phones can't. Every change goes in the lab's record (**Activity**, **Everyone**).
  - **How chats see it:** every chat's prompt carries a short brief of it (lessons first, then wiring, references, excitations and other facts, within a fixed length), with the measurement chain on record. Kelvin looks up the rest when it needs it. A note tied to an instrument is shown when that instrument is used (when Kelvin reads its card, and the first time a chat sets it or runs on it), and its lessons on the approval cards that name it.
  - **How Kelvin adds to it:** when you tell it such a fact, or a run shows one, Kelvin keeps it and says so in the chat. A chat that has read text from outside the lab since you last wrote (a web page, a paper, a file in its folder) asks you first, since a note reaches every chat. Chats in unpublished, confidential or model-limited projects read the lab's memory but add nothing to it; with **Learn from conversations** off, Kelvin adds nothing either. Incognito chats neither read nor add to it.
  - **Where it is:** `~/Kelvin/lab-memory.json`. A file someone broke by hand is left alone: Kelvin keeps nothing in it until it is fixed.
- **Skills and recipes:** know-how that worked, kept at the same levels (yours for every project, or one project's).
  - **A skill** is a procedure in words: cooling down, aligning a laser through the objective, how this lab finds the Dirac point. Kelvin reads one when a task matches it and follows it unless you say otherwise. Kelvin comes with a few general ones: setting up an instrument, lock-in measurements, cool-downs, finding a resonance, I–V curves, checking a result.
  - **Packed as Claude's skills are:** a folder with `SKILL.md` (its name and when to use it, then the steps) and the files that go with it: a calibration table, a reference figure, a maker's sequence file. Kelvin reads those files when the steps call for them (`read_skill_file`), and never runs a skill's scripts. **Add a skill…** (Settings → **Memory**, or a project's settings) takes a skill made for Claude, as a .zip or .skill file or a lone SKILL.md; **Download** gives one of Kelvin's to use in Claude or another Kelvin. Add only skills from people you trust: a skill tells Kelvin what to do.
  - **Starting one yourself:** type / at the start of a message for the list, and pick one: `/cool-down from 300 K` gives Kelvin that skill's steps with your message, and `/find-gate-peak` runs that recipe.
  - **Switching one off:** each skill and recipe has a switch. Off, Kelvin neither sees nor uses it, and it stays kept. Kelvin's own can be switched off too.
  - **A recipe** is a measurement in stages that checks its own results: each stage a plan, a check on its data (`max(counter.rate) > 2e4`), values kept for the next stage (`at_min(counter.rate, mw.frequency)`), and how often to try again with changed values. Kelvin runs one by its name, every stage through the safety gate like any plan ([data.md](data.md#recipes)).
  - **Keeping them:** when a procedure or a staged measurement worked and will be needed again, Kelvin keeps it and says so in the chat. See, read and delete them in Settings → **Memory** (every project) and in a project's settings. Work in an unshared project keeps what it learned in that project. Incognito chats see only Kelvin's own skills.
- **Confidential projects:** In a project's **Project settings → Who may see this project**, tick **Confidential**: only local models may read it, literature and web search are off, and nothing of it goes through the Kelvin hub. Below it you can still choose which kinds of model may read it (Claude, GPT, Gemini, local models), and whether literature and web search may be used.
  - **At a glance:** a lock marks such a project and its chats in the sidebar, and the composer shows **Confidential** (or **Unpublished**, or **No search** for a project with only search off); point at it to see what it means.
  - **Enforcement:** Kelvin refuses any other model for chats, comparisons, second opinions and the master report's opening (**Write the opening with Kelvin**), and leaves out the plan reviewer if it isn't allowed. Confidential work never uses a model through the Kelvin hub.
  - **Its runs stay inside it:** only the project's own chats see its runs, its sample's name and its part of the lab record, and only with the models its rules allow. Other chats, incognito chats, second opinions and Compare lanes with other models don't, nor do day reports and lab notebook records made from another chat ([data.md](data.md#your-data)). A run started in another chat is never filed under its mounted sample.
  - **The rules stay with the chat:** a chat that ran under them keeps them, even if it is moved out of the project or the project is deleted, so its history never reaches a model you excluded.
- **Kelvin hub:** the hub is one server for Kelvin users in every lab ([hub.md](hub.md)). In Settings → **Account**, under Kelvin hub, type the hub's address and press **Connect**. Kelvin opens the hub in your browser, where you sign in (or register) with your hub password and choose the lab to share into.
  - **What is shared:** your normal projects and unfiled chats, with their whole conversations, and the runs you made in them or outside every project (run.h5 and reports), with the members of that lab. Kelvin sends what changed every two minutes, and what you delete goes from the hub too. **Sync now** sends it at once. If the hub fails on one file (a time-out on a large one, say), the others go on and that file waits: 5 minutes, then twice as long each time, up to 4 hours. Hover over the status line to see what the hub didn't take.
  - **Never shared:** projects marked **Unpublished** or **Confidential** under **Project settings → On the Kelvin hub**, not even their names; projects limited to some models or without literature search; incognito chats and the runs they started; anyone else's runs, even in a data folder you share; API keys; ChatGPT and Claude Code sign-ins. Marking a project takes away what the hub had of it.
  - **Marks stay with chats:** a chat moved out of an unpublished, confidential or model-limited project, or left when the project is deleted, stays unshared. It keeps the limits the project has then, and so does a chat that ran under them, even if they are lifted later. Changing a project's limits doesn't lock chats that didn't run under them. Runs started by a chat that keeps such a mark are never shared, wherever they are filed.
  - **No leaks through notes or samples:** while a chat is in such a project, it keeps no notes for every conversation (those reach shared chats). A run that it starts is filed in its project, without the sample mounted from another project, and another chat's run is never filed under its sample.
  - **Older runs:** runs from before this version of Kelvin, and runs of scans queued before it, don't record who made them or whether an incognito chat started them, so they stay on your computer. Tick **Also share older runs** to share them too, but only if your data folder is yours alone. The status line says how many stay.
  - **One connection each:** everyone on a computer connects as their own Kelvin, so two people (or two accounts of yours) keep their own connections. The hub shows the account name on its connect page. Connecting again, after an update for example, moves what you shared before to the new connection, so the hub keeps one copy.
  - **The token:** Kelvin never sees your hub password. Its token is kept in your settings, encrypted with your password, and never shown in the browser. Kelvin never follows a redirect with it.
  - **Disconnect** stops sharing at once, even in the middle of a sync. What was shared stays on the hub until you delete it on your account page there (**Your account on the hub**).
- **Incognito:** For a question that has nothing to do with your work, press **Incognito chat** in the top bar (from Home it shows the chat); from a saved chat it starts a new incognito one. While it is on, its button and the message box have a dashed outline. Kelvin then uses no memory at all (not yours, a project's or the lab's) and keeps none, and the chat is never saved or listed; it is gone when you leave it. Press the button again to leave it, and the chat you were in before comes back. Once it holds messages, leaving asks first (**Forget this chat?**), and so does opening another chat or **New chat**; **Cancel** keeps it. Closing the page forgets it too, and a reload never starts in it. The lab's instruments and their limits still apply, and an approval card still shows you the lab's lessons on the instruments it names. Instrument commands and runs are still logged, for safety.
- **Literature:** Kelvin searches arXiv and journal papers (through [OpenAlex](https://openalex.org)) and reads them, so it can plan an experiment from what is known and check a result against published work. Try "what parameters did people use to drive magnons with SAWs in CrSBr? propose a plan".
  - **Search:** results come with authors, venue, citation counts and abstracts.
  - **Reading:** arXiv papers with an HTML version are read in full, section by section, with equations as LaTeX; other papers give their abstract.
  - **Citations:** Kelvin cites what it used as [Author Year](link) and never invents a reference.
  - **Reading list:** in a project, ask Kelvin to keep a paper. It goes on the project's reading list (Project settings), Kelvin sees that list in the project's conversations, and reports list it as references.
  - **Privacy:** only the search words go to arXiv and OpenAlex, never your data or chats. Switch literature and web search off in Settings for confidential work. A free OpenAlex API key (Settings) raises the daily search allowance.
- **The web:** Kelvin searches the web (`web_search`) for what it needs: an instrument's manual or programming guide, its maker's pages, software, drivers and documentation. It reads pages and PDFs (`read_web_page`, with each page's links, documents first) and keeps a manual (`save_manual`). A manual kept for an instrument goes into its folder when Kelvin adds it, and Kelvin writes the instrument's card from it.
  - **Search:** through the web search your model service already offers, so you need no other account. Kelvin uses Claude's web search with a Claude API key, OpenAI's with an OpenAI key, Google Search with a Gemini key, or Claude Code's WebSearch through the Claude Code bridge. It tries the chat's own model first, then any other that can search. With a Claude API key, an admin may need to allow web search in the Claude Console.
  - **Privacy:** only the search words and the pages Kelvin opens leave the computer. The same switch as literature search turns it off, in Settings and per project.
- **Several models at once:** Switch on **Compare models** at the top of Settings → **Models** (it is off by default, and acts at once): **Compare** then shows next to the model picker. Press it and tick one or two more models. Each message then goes to all of them, and the answers stream side by side. While comparing, the models can read the lab (status, values, runs, dry runs) but not change it, so three models never drive the same instruments. Each answer says which model gave it, at its foot (every other answer is headed only **Kelvin**). Press **Continue with this answer** under the one you prefer; the conversation goes on from that answer and with that model.
- **Second opinions:** A different model checks Kelvin's work.
  - **Plans:** pick a reviewer in Settings → **Second opinion on plans**. It then reads every plan before it runs: the request, the lab's state, Kelvin's own checks, and the plan. It answers go, caution or stop with its findings, shown under the plan's step. If it says stop, Kelvin asks you before running the plan, with the findings as reasons.
  - **Answers:** an answer has no **Second opinion** button any more (since 2026-11-03): only Copy is under it. A second opinion an answer got before still shows under it, saying whether the reviewer agreed, partly agreed or disagreed, with its findings.
  - **Checked, not trusted:** the reviewer looks up what the answer or the plan rests on, in the lab's records: the runs' data and descriptions, computing from their columns, traces and plots, the instruments' descriptions and manuals, the lab's state and log (up to 10 lookups). Each finding says how serious it is, how sure the reviewer is from what it checked, and the evidence. Only findings it is at least 70% sure of are shown and passed on to Kelvin; the others stay folded under the card. A stop or a disagree needs a sure, serious finding; otherwise it counts as caution or partly.
  - **Re-check:** under an answer's second opinion, **Re-check the findings with another model** has a second model check each finding against the records too. Findings it confirms are marked; findings it can't confirm are set aside.
  - **Limits:** the reviewer only reads: it can't change anything, run a plan or take a camera frame. Its advice doesn't replace the safety gate's limits or approvals. A model from another company gives a more independent view.
- **Pictures:** Kelvin can look at data as a picture, the way you glance at a plot to judge a peak's shape, noise, a jump or where a spot sits (`look_at`): a run's plots as its report draws them, a 2-D scan as a color map, a camera frame from a run or read now. The picture shows under the step. Models that see pictures get it with the caption; others get the caption, which gives the numbers. The latest 8 pictures go along at each step; for values Kelvin uses the numbers, not the pixels.
- **Which models see pictures:** Claude, Gemini, GPT through an OpenAI key and the ChatGPT plan all do. On other services (OpenRouter, OpenCode, DeepSeek, a local server) Kelvin decides model by model (`web/vision.py`), by the first of these that knows:
  - the service's **Pictures** choice, when it is **On** or **Off**;
  - the model refused a picture on this computer in the last 30 days;
  - the service's own model list, where it says (OpenRouter's does);
  - the Kelvin hub's list, for a person signed in to the hub (the hub reads [models.dev](https://models.dev) once a day; [hub.md](hub.md#for-signed-in-kelvins));
  - otherwise Kelvin sends them. A model that refuses gets the same message without them, and Kelvin says so under the answer. When the service says the model takes no pictures, Kelvin remembers that for 30 days.
- **Attachments:** attach photos, screenshots, PDFs, data files or text files with the paperclip, or paste or drop them into the message box: a photo of the wiring, an oscilloscope's screen, a paper behind a paywall, a cycler's export. A data file (CSV, Excel, NumPy, TIFF, BioLogic, Gamry or Arbin) becomes a run as you attach it, and its chip says how many points or frames it has ([Data from other programs](data.md#data-from-other-programs)). Big photos are shrunk to what models read before they are sent. A text file (a script, a Jupyter notebook without its saved outputs, notes, settings; up to 2 MB) goes to every model as its text; nothing runs it. A PDF of up to 20 pages goes whole, figures and all, to a model that reads PDFs (Claude, GPT through an OpenAI key, Gemini); otherwise, and for a longer one, as its text. For a model that sees pictures but can't read PDFs, the Kelvin hub also draws the PDF's pages with figures as pictures (the first 6), once, when you send it; they go with its text. That needs you signed in to the hub, and never happens in incognito chats or unpublished, confidential or model-limited projects: there, attach a picture of a figure that matters. Either way it is marked for the model as text from outside the lab ([safety.md](safety.md#text-from-outside-the-lab)). A model that can't see pictures (most local models) gets only each picture's name, and Kelvin says so when you send one. Attachments are kept with the chat and go when it is deleted.
- **Files in the chat's folder:** Kelvin lists and reads the files in the folder you chose for the chat (`list_folder`, `read_file`), and nothing outside it: PDFs by pages, text and CSV files, and pictures. A long file is read in parts, and Kelvin reads on where it stopped. A PDF goes to the model as its text, or for the figures, its pages themselves (up to 20 at once) when the model reads PDFs: Claude, GPT through an OpenAI key, and Gemini. For a model that sees pictures but can't read PDFs, the Kelvin hub draws the pages as pictures (6 at once), as for attached PDFs; other models get the text. What Kelvin reads there is marked as text from outside the lab, and pauses auto mode until you write again ([safety.md](safety.md#text-from-outside-the-lab)). Kelvin also saves text files there (`write_file`), such as a script it wrote for you, notes or a table, without asking; a file it replaces is kept first in `.kelvin-old` beside it. Kelvin's own folder (`~/Kelvin`: lab config, settings, accounts, cards, everyone's chats) is never read or written this way, even from a chat whose folder contains it, and can't be chosen as a chat's folder; the data folder (everyone's runs and the lab's records) is never read this way, nor a run of another chat's unshared work kept in a chat's folder (a listing counts it without its name); nor is a file that may hold sign-ins, keys or tokens (`.ssh`, `.env`, a key file, other programs' sign-ins in Windows' `AppData`), nor are the lab's records rewritten ([safety.md](safety.md#the-rules)). Python code in a folder an instrument's library loads from shows in the chat whole, since it runs on the instrument ([safety.md](safety.md#the-rules)).
- **Writing while Kelvin works:** you don't have to wait for its answer. Type and send: the button shows **Stop** while the box is empty. What you send reaches Kelvin after the step it is on (a run or a wait finishes first; **Stop** stops it now) and stands in the chat where Kelvin read it. If Kelvin had already finished, it goes as your next message. A message that only says stop (`STOP`, `stop!`, `stop now`, `abort`, `/stop`) is not passed on: it stops Kelvin's answer and the lab's work at once, as the Stop button does, whoever started it.
- **Several chats at once:** while Kelvin answers in one chat, open another (or a new one) and ask there; up to 4 chats answer side by side. A dot marks a chat that is answering, has a new answer, or (amber) asks for your approval, and a note at the bottom says when an answer or a request comes in a chat you left (click it to go there). Going back to a chat still answering shows its answer as it goes.
  - **Changes go one at a time:** a set, a run, a raw command, starting the queue or adding an instrument is one chat's step at a time, filed under that chat (its runs go to its folder and project). A chat that needs to change the lab while another's step does waits a few seconds (not at all while a longer run, the queue or an approval card holds the lab), then says what holds it, and the model tells you.
  - **Everything else goes on beside it, from any chat:** looking things up (runs and their plots, even the one running; cards, experiments, manuals), reading the instruments, the queue (adding, editing, reordering, pausing; an item is filed under the chat that queued it) and watches. A reading never sends a command to an instrument the run is using, as one in between could upset what the run does there (a lock-in's acquisition, a sweep the instrument takes). Kelvin gives that instrument's latest values instead, with how old each is and whether the run measured or set it, and reads the other instruments as usual.
  - **Stop:** the button stops the chat you are in, and the lab's work too when it is that chat's. Asking Kelvin to stop in any chat (it calls `stop_run`), or typing `STOP`, stops the lab's work whoever started it.
- **Tools when needed:** Kelvin keeps its everyday tools at hand, and loads the rarer ones (setting up instruments and writing cards, installing makers' software, editing the queue, watches, pulse boards, SQL on the catalog) when a chat needs them (`find_tools`), so each step reads less and answers sooner. Lookups that touch no instrument (runs, papers, the web, files) run side by side; changes to the instruments go one at a time, and readings beside them.
- **Waiting:** for a long wait (a cool-down, a temperature to settle, a long queue), Kelvin ends its answer and continues the chat later by itself (`wake_me`): after a while, once a reading holds a condition for some minutes, or when the run or queue ends. Meanwhile you can use Kelvin in other chats. A bar above the message box says when it will continue, with **Don't continue**, and a notification says when it did. A wait inside an answer is at most 2 minutes. A chat is continued at most 12 times a day, only while you are signed in, and never an incognito chat. A wait goes on after Kelvin restarts (an update, say); a wait for a run that the restart stopped ends, and Kelvin looks at how the run ended.
- **Long conversations:** when a conversation grows close to what the model can read at once, Kelvin summarises the earlier part for the model as a lab handoff (what you want, the lab's state, the runs with their IDs and results, decisions, open questions) and keeps the last two exchanges as they are. A note in the chat says so. The chat itself keeps everything, and Kelvin searches it when it needs an earlier detail (`chat_history`).
- **Conversation:** Answers stream in Markdown. Each tool call is a step on one line: a click on it shows its input and result under it, and a second click hides them. Anything that drives the sample harder appears as an **Approve / Decline** card inside the conversation; once answered, it shrinks to one line (what it asked, and the answer), whose click shows the details again.
- **Conversation list:** the **Conversations** button (≡) is at the left of the top bar at every window size, also in full screen. It is the only thing that hides or shows the list of projects and conversations; opening panels never hides it. Where the window has room, the list slides away and the chat and the side panels take its room. On a narrow window (a phone, for example) the list takes the place of the chat and the panels below the top bar; choosing a chat, **Chat** or **Home**, a panel's button or ≡ again brings back what was there. A narrow window always opens on the chat or Home, never on the list. In full screen the button ends full screen and shows the list. Kelvin remembers whether it is shown, per browser.
- **Side panels:** Team chat, Analysis, Runs, the scan editor and Lab each open and close on their own. They stand side by side beside the chat in the order of the top bar's buttons: Team chat, Analysis, Runs, Lab, with the scan editor (Edit on a scan opens it) right after Runs. Each keeps its own width (drag its left edge; Kelvin remembers it). With less room they narrow alike; when one more will not fit beside the chat even so, the panel you looked at least recently (opened, clicked or typed in) is put away: its button goes flat, and it shows the same again when you open it. A panel that opens by itself (a run starting, Kelvin's analysis) never puts away the one you are typing in, and under full screen or the conversation list it waits out of sight. At 1280×720 with the list shown, Runs stands beside Lab or Analysis, and the scan editor takes Runs' place; to make more room, hide the conversation list with ≡. A panel that cannot stand beside the chat at all (on a phone, for example, or the scan editor on a touch screen, where it needs 800 px) takes the chat's place on its own, and **Chat** or **Home** brings the chat back. No panel ever opens over or under another.
- **Opening panels:** a panel's top-bar button is raised exactly when that panel is on the screen. The button puts its panel away when it is on the screen, and otherwise brings it there. Everything else that opens a panel (a Home tile, Analyze on a run, Edit on a scan, a request waiting for an answer) only shows it. If the panel is already on the screen, its header lights up briefly instead, and nothing closes.
- **Remembered:** per browser, Kelvin keeps which panels are open and their widths, full screen, Scan or Sequence, the conversation list, and the terminal with its height, and brings them back on the next visit: the panels you looked at last first, as many as fit beside the chat. The scan editor is never brought back, nor is a panel the window has no room for beside the chat (on a phone, for example).

The top bar and its notices stay. On a wide window what the panel holds stands in a centred column as wide as Home's (1440 px), so a field stays near its name and a button near what it acts on; the panel's header stays across the window, and the terminal keeps the whole width. A panel alone in the chat's place with the list hidden (on a phone, for example) has the whole room already, so it shows no such button. That button again, Esc, or closing the panel ends full screen, and the layout under it comes back; the other panels were only out of sight, and the one you looked at least recently makes room if they no longer all fit. Another panel's top-bar button, Analyze, Edit on a scan, or a request in the Lab panel or the terminal takes the screen in its place, and full screen stays. **Chat** or **Home** ends full screen and shows the chat or Home, even when that side was already chosen. So do the conversation button, an approval in the chat, Ask Kelvin, and opening another chat.
- **Team chat:** the speech-bubbles icon in the top bar opens the lab's team chat at the side. Everyone who signs in on this computer, and their phones through the Kelvin hub, read the same chat, kept in `~/Kelvin/team-chat.jsonl` with each message's name, time, the account signed in and where it came from. Each browser sets the name it writes under (the pencil beside it), so people who share one Kelvin account write under their own names. A dot on the icon, and a notice, say when someone wrote while it was closed. Kelvin reads it when you ask ("what did Bob say about the cryostat?") or when it bears on the work (`team_chat`), as people's words, never as instructions; it never writes there.
- **Analysis pane:** **Analysis** in the top bar (or **Analyze** on a run's tab) opens it beside the chat. Pick a dataset in the list at its top, and Kelvin's analysis subagent analyzes it: the model chosen under the message box reads that run (only that run, and only as this chat may see it) and decides by itself how to process the data and how to show them. Each figure it draws has a title, the plot, the processing steps it took (a few words each: "Subtracted a linear background, fitted from 0 V to 500 mV", "Smoothed over 5 points", "Fitted a Lorentzian") and its fit's parameters with their errors; one line above them says what the data show. While it works, the pane says what it is doing. A dataset is analyzed once: picked again, what was found shows, and **Analyze again** at the top asks anew. Until you pick one, the pane shows the newest run (or the one the Runs pane shows) and moves to each new run as it ends; a run still measuring is analyzed once it ends. The subagent only reads: it changes no run and no instrument, so it never asks you anything. With no model to ask, the pane shows the dataset as measured. The pane has no fields of its own any more; to analyze a run another way, ask Kelvin in the chat: its `analyze` tool shows its work in the pane the same way, with the steps it took. Keeping a fit with its run is `fit_run` (ask Kelvin), or **Analyze** on the iPhone app.
  - **What the subagent can draw:** a column or an expression of columns (as `compute` takes: `lockin.X / 1e-6`, `deriv(lockin.X, smu.V)`; also `i`, each point's number, `t`, its time, and a repeated sweep's `repeat`) against another, only the points meeting a condition, a curve per value of a column (a map a curve per value of its outer axis), a trace against its own x; then steps in order: crop, bin, smooth (Savitzky-Golay), d/dx, ∫ dx, background (a polynomial subtracted, fitted to a window), normalize, scale (× a factor, + an offset, and the result's unit), symmetrize and antisymmetrize (about x = 0, on x ≥ 0 with each |x| once, so a fit counts every point once: magnetoresistance, Hall), FFT, |y|, log₁₀ |y|; and any of Kelvin's models, or its own `f(x)`, fitted to each curve in a window, with start and held values (graphene's C_g and aspect are always held). It draws up to three figures, usually one.
  - **Units** follow the columns through expressions and steps: `lockin.X / smu.I` is in Ω, `lockin.X / 1e-6` in µV, d/dx of a field sweep in V/T, a normalized curve × 100 in %. Where a number may carry a unit of its own (`lockin.X / 2e-8`, the volts over a current), the pane says the unit is unknown: give it in the scale step.
  - **The steps it took** are said from the figure it drew, not from the model's own words: each step with the names and units the curve had at that step (a crop's ends in x's unit then), and the fit's window.

## From LabVIEW

A lab that runs its experiments from LabVIEW switches to Kelvin directly: **Lab** → **Set up…** → **From your LabVIEW…** reads the lab's LabVIEW and sets Kelvin up the way it already works. No VI is run, LabVIEW needn't be installed, and nothing is sent to an instrument but the question who it is.
- **What it reads:** NI MAX on this computer (NI-VISA's aliases, the names your lab gives its instruments, and the resources it lists; NI-DAQmx devices from a configuration exported from MAX into the folder), and in the folder you choose, each LabVIEW project (`.lvproj`: its VIs, libraries, drivers, and hardware targets such as a CompactRIO) and each VI. A copy of the lab computer's `visaconf.ini` in the folder is read too, so you can try it on another computer.
- **Instruments:** the same review as **Scripts…**: each instrument with its address, its name in NI MAX, the card that fits (a model the alias or a driver library names, such as *Keithley 2400 Series*; the instrument's own answer decides), its settings and the limits from the values its VIs use. Tick the ones you want and **Add the checked ones to the lab config**; the limits are marked "from your LabVIEW VIs", for you to check.
- **Scans:** each measurement VI Kelvin could read becomes a scan: its controls' saved values, its loops as sweeps, its waits after a set as settle times, its readings as the measured values, what it does before and after the loops as setup and after. **How it translates** shows what each part of the VI became, and **Not translated** says plainly what didn't: state machines, event structures, parallel loops, Call Library nodes, unknown sub-VIs, a wait anywhere else. Nothing is guessed. Tick the VIs and **Keep the checked VIs as scans**: they go to the **Sequence**'s functions (**Runs** → **Sequence**, **＋ Add** → Scans), marked LabVIEW, and run through the safety gate like any scan.
- **Translate:** every VI Kelvin read, an app with modes too, has **Translate**: the AI writes it as one of the lab's experiments (Python that reaches the instruments only through Kelvin's safety gate, one action per mode or per button the VI runs, the controls a person changes as it runs as live inputs, every line tagged with the VI node it comes from), and Kelvin checks it with fixed rules and rehearses each action on a simulated copy of the lab, sending what fails back, before you see it. It takes a few minutes, and the VI goes only to a model your project allows. The review shows **What blocks it** (a path around the safety gate, a drive the VI never touched, an input that isn't the VI's saved value, a rehearsal that breaks a limit or never ends: **Keep** waits until nothing does), each action's rehearsal, the warnings (keeping it accepts them), what wasn't translated and whose list that is, what keeping it would add to the lab's LabVIEW map, and the code. Name it and **Keep**: it is the lab's, in the **Sequence**'s functions (**Runs** → **Sequence**, **＋ Add** → Experiments), for everyone who signs in here.
- **One VI on its own:** **Runs** → **Sequence** → **＋ New function** and choose the VI (in a browser, type its full path), give it a name, then **Add**. A path Kelvin can't read is said there, with the path kept to fix. Otherwise Kelvin starts translating it at once, as **Translate** does, and its review opens when it is done (**Cancel** or Esc right after **Add** lets it go on all the same, with no dialog). You can close it meanwhile: the translation goes on, listed among the Scan's experiments as translating (or, if it fails, with why and × to let it go), and a note says when it is done; a click on the note opens its review. Its draft then waits among the experiments with **Review**, after a reload too, until it is kept or let go: its × deletes it, after a yes (a draft that can't be kept stays listed until then, its review saying why), and once kept it is there as an experiment. Kelvin looks for the VI's own sub-VIs in its folder; nothing is copied, and the VI is only read.
- **In a chat:** ask Kelvin to look at your LabVIEW folder (`survey_labview`), describe a VI (`describe_vi`) or translate it (`translate_vi`). Its AI never keeps scans in your library; you do, here. A translation it makes is kept only when a person says yes, on its card or here.
- **Old data:** LabVIEW's measurement files (`.lvm`) import as runs ([data.md](data.md#data-from-other-programs)), to compare a scan's first run with an earlier LabVIEW one.

## From Special Measure

A lab that runs Special Measure (the MATLAB program with a rack of named channels, scans and a queue) switches to Kelvin directly: **Lab** → **Set up…** → **From Special Measure…** reads its folder and sets Kelvin up the way the rack already is. No MATLAB is run (it needn't be installed), and nothing is sent to an instrument but the question who it is.
- **What it reads:** in the folder you choose (your Special Measure folder: its setup scripts, `sm`, its instruments and your data), each setup script that builds a rack (`smloadinst`, `smaddchannel`), read as Special Measure would run it: the flags it sets first (`LockIn_Use = 1`) decide which instruments it loads. Each model's driver (`smc*.m`) says which command each of its channels sends and what it does with the value (a PEM's wavelength times its retardance, a position over 20 minus 5 V), and its `sminst` file the channels' order and the numbers the driver uses. An instrument whose driver writes a DAQ session (MATLAB's Data Acquisition Toolbox, made by the script its block runs) is the NI DAQ device it writes: Kelvin's NI DAQ card, each channel the analog output it writes, with that conversion. Where Kelvin's card takes a channel in its own unit (MultiVu's field in tesla, which Special Measure sends in Oe as 10000 times the value), the multiplier is left out. Scans saved from Special Measure's scan editor, and those in your newest runs (one per kind), are read too.
- **Instruments:** the same review as **Scripts…**, for the setup script you pick (the newest at first): each instrument with its address (its GPIB board and address, its serial port, or the instrument object its block opens itself), the card that fits (*K2400* is a Keithley 2400; the instrument's own answer decides), and its channels: each with the card parameter it is (*THETA_A → theta*), its limits, ramp rate and multiplier. An instrument the script doesn't load (its flag is 0) is listed unticked. **Add the checked ones to the lab config** adds them with the channels' limits, marked "from your Special Measure rack" for you to check (apart from the card's own limits, for you to tighten), the ramp rates of the drives Kelvin ramps itself (at most the card's maximum), and the channels' names as the lab's own names (`names:` in `lab.yaml`): plans, the scan editor, the chat and `set_value` then take `Vbg` for `dac.CH0`.
- **Scans:** each scan becomes a Kelvin scan by fixed rules: its loops outer first; a loop's first channel steps, the others move with it (**+ Change another** in the scan editor); its wait settles each point and its start wait the first; what the inner loop records at each point, what an outer loop records once per value; its constants as setup; an escape function that sets channels to numbers as what the scan sets at the end and after a stop; function channels (a density and a field for two gates) as variables with **Computed** settings, their arguments in the Function's channel order, and constants on a Function no loop sweeps as the settings its formulas give. A reading Special Measure divides by its channel's multiplier is recorded as the instrument reads it, and said. Anything else (a `trafofn`, a loop the instrument ramps itself, a function whose formula uses the setup's workspace) is said under **Not translated**, and nothing is guessed. Tick them and **Keep the checked scans**: they go to the scans in **Runs** → **Scan**, marked Special Measure, and run through the safety gate like any scan.
- **Runs:** your run files (`NNN_name.mat`), by folder: **Import** brings a folder's runs in, each a run of its own with its number, name, comments and the day it was taken, up to 500 at a time (Import again for more). One imported already is left as it is.
- **In a chat:** ask Kelvin to look at your Special Measure folder (`survey_specialmeasure`). Its AI never keeps scans in your library; you do, here.

## Calibration

Kelvin keeps each instrument's calibration as you record it: **Lab** → **Set up…** → **Calibration…** on the instrument's row (instruments on this computer, not simulated ones).
- **What you record:** when it was calibrated, when it is due again, the certificate's number, who calibrated it, and the certificate itself (a PDF, or a photo of it), kept in the instrument's folder (`~/Kelvin/instruments/<name>/calibration.json` and `certificates/`). The serial number is the one the instrument gives in its identity reply. Every record is kept, so a run's record says which calibration was in force when it ran.
- **Its row** says where it stands: no calibration on record, calibrated and due on a date, due within 30 days, or **out of date** since a date.
- **When one is out of date,** `health_check` says so for that instrument, and so does every approval card that names it (*Calibration out of date: dmm was due 2026-09-30 (certificate C-1234)*), kept with the card in the lab record. It is a warning, not a stop: the instrument works, and its readings say what they say. `health_check` also says when one is due within 30 days, and a card when the instrument now reports another serial number than the calibration's (another unit at that address).
- **Kelvin** reads it (`instrument_info` says the calibration on record) but never records one: that is a person's, and in the lab record with their name.

## Terminal

The **Terminal** button in the top bar (or Ctrl+`) opens a console at the foot of the window. There you can type to Kelvin, or send an instrument its own commands.
- **Talking to Kelvin:** type as in a chat. Kelvin's steps show as `●` lines with their results below, a run's progress counts up, and its data appear live in the Runs pane. The conversation is saved like any other chat.
- **An instrument's own commands:** `/use lockin` (or just `/lockin`) sends what you type to that instrument, such as `*IDN?`, `SENS?` or `:SOUR:VOLT 1`. Queries show the reply; anything else shows what it did. `/kelvin` goes back to talking to Kelvin.
- **The same safety gate:** limits, interlocks and blocked commands apply as for Kelvin. A change that drives the sample harder asks **approve? [y/N]** right in the terminal. Moving back toward safe doesn't ask. A terminal command never lands in the middle of a run: while Kelvin is busy it is refused, not queued. Every command is in the lab record as yours (`human (terminal)`), and Kelvin's **undo** never undoes it.
- **Other commands:**
  - `/ls`: the instruments.
  - `/status [name]`: readings, outputs and settings.
  - `/get X`, `/set time_constant 300 ms`, `/set smu.V 0.5`: read or set by name.
  - `/describe [name]`: units, limits and safe values.
  - `/manual <words>`: search the instrument's manual.
  - `/new`: start a new conversation with Kelvin.
  - `/stop`: stop Kelvin's answer, any ramp or run, and the queue.
  - `/clear`: clear the screen.
- **Keys:** Up and Down recall what you typed. Tab completes commands, instrument names and parameters. Esc stops Kelvin's answer.

## Updates and one Kelvin per computer

Kelvin keeps itself up to date. Twice a day it looks for a newer version, downloads it, checks it against the checksum GitHub gives for the file, and installs it when you close Kelvin. **Restart to update**, in the notice row under the top bar, installs it at once. It never installs while a plan, the queue or a chat is running. Settings → **Updates** chooses Automatic, Ask me first, or Off for everyone on the computer, and has **Check now**. On macOS Kelvin says when a new version is out and links to it.

There is only ever one Kelvin on a computer: an update or a new installer replaces the installed one in place, keeping your settings, chats and data. If you start another copy (say, one unzipped somewhere), Kelvin says where the installed one is, so you can delete the extra copy.

## Phone and remote access

Check on a run from the coffee room, or answer an approval from home, with **Kelvin for iPhone** signed in with your [Kelvin account](#kelvin-accounts-one-account-every-computer). It finds every Kelvin where you are signed in with that account, and reaches each one in one of two ways:

- **Directly, on the lab network:** switch on **Phone access** in Settings → **Phone** (it is off until you do). Kelvin then also listens on the network (port 8771 by default), and the phone gets its own token for it through the Kelvin hub. Directly is faster, and it is the only way unshared work (unpublished, confidential or model-limited projects, incognito chats and their runs) opens on the phone. With [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com) on the computer and the phone, this works away from the lab too.
- **Through the Kelvin hub, from anywhere else:** while you are signed in on the Kelvin computer, it keeps a connection out to the hub, and the phone goes through it when no direct address answers ([hub.md](hub.md#phones-through-the-hub)). Firewalls and Wi-Fi that keeps devices apart don't stop it. Unshared work never goes this way. Settings → **Account** says whether your phone can reach this Kelvin now, and switches it off.

**If the phone doesn't connect directly on the lab network:** the computer's firewall must let it in. On Windows, press **Let phones through** in Settings → Phone: Windows asks you to allow it. Many university, housing and hotel Wi-Fi networks keep devices apart; there the phone goes through the hub.

**If this computer's address is public** (as on many campus networks), the iPhone connects through the hub, even on the lab's Wi-Fi; [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com) on both gives the direct connection that opens unshared work.

Settings → Phone lists the phones that reach Kelvin directly, with when each was last seen. Remove one there, or on the phone: swipe left on the computer in **Computers**. A phone paired with an earlier Kelvin keeps working, with what it was allowed then, until you remove it. Your desktop's access key never works on the network; only a phone's own token does. Settings, instruments, the lab config and the approval mode work only on the Kelvin computer.

Each person's phones are their own: a phone works while its person is signed in on the Kelvin computer, and sees that person's chats and runs, never someone else's.

The app's tabs:

- **Lab:** alarms, requests waiting for you, the run in progress with a live plot and **Stop**, the instruments (drives not at their safe value in orange), recent notices.
- **Chat:** your conversations, a new or incognito chat, the answer as it arrives with Kelvin's steps, and requests to approve right there. Tap the microphone to talk instead of typing: the iPhone turns your words into text itself, the audio never leaves it, and you check the message before sending.
- **Team:** the lab's team chat, the same as on the computer. Set the name you write under once on the phone. A badge counts the messages written since you last looked.
- **Runs:** recent runs with their plots, and their report and data to open on the phone. **Analyze** opens the run in the phone's Analysis: more runs over it, x and y (a column or a formula), the steps, a fit with its errors, **Keep fit** and the CSV to share (the computer's Analysis pane has its analysis subagent instead).
- **Computers:** your Kelvin account and its Kelvins (one row per computer: of the Kelvins a computer has signed in with, the one online, else the latest), switch between computers, swipe left to remove one from the phone (**Look for my Kelvins** brings it back), sign out.

Team and Analysis need Kelvin 0.3.32 or later on the lab computer.

Without a lab computer at hand, **Try a demo lab** shows a made-up one inside the app. The app tells you about new requests and alarms while it is open. For alerts while it is closed, add an email account or an ntfy topic under Notifications. A request nobody answers at the computer comes to the phone, and waits there for you (Settings → Notifications). Building the app: [ios.md](ios.md).

Directly, traffic on a plain Wi-Fi network isn't encrypted: on a shared network, use Tailscale, or switch Phone access off so the phone goes through the hub. Don't forward Kelvin's port to the internet.
