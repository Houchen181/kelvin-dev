# Safety

Every command goes through one safety gate, whoever sends it: Kelvin, another agent, or a person in the terminal.

## The rules

Every command passes through the `SafetyGate`, both direct commands (`get`, `set`, `raw`) and the steps of a plan.

| Command | What happens |
|---|---|
| Measure / configure | Runs immediately and is logged |
| Drive toward safe | Runs, ramped at the lab's rate |
| Drive away from safe | Limits and interlocks are checked, then a human is asked (unless the project envelope, an approved plan or auto mode covers it). The change is ramped while guard readings are watched, and it goes back to safe if one trips |
| Beyond the lab's limits or the mounted device's, or a blocked command | Refused |

- Raw strings are matched against the card, each part of a `;` chain on its own (a chain the card itself writes as one setting, such as an AMI 430's `CONF:FIELD:TARG {value};RAMP`, is checked as that setting). A raw string that sets a drive is treated as a drive, and one that changes a source's range follows the range rule below. Blocked and reset families come from the card.
  - **In a drive's family but not its plain setting** (a sweep's stop value, a mode): refused when the drive has an interlock, since the interlock can't be checked against it, or when the number it carries is beyond the drive's limits. Otherwise a person decides, and sees that number against the limits.
  - **Never sent unless the card lists them** (as a parameter, which is then checked with its limits, or an action): the source function (`:SOUR:FUNC CURR`), protection limits and compliance (`…:PROT`, `…:ILIM`), the units readings are in (`UNIT…`, `CONF:FIELD:UNITS`), and calibration (`CAL…`, `*CAL?`).
  - **Anything else not on the card**, on an instrument with drives, asks a person first: Kelvin can't tell what it does to the sample. Only status housekeeping (`*CLS`, `*WAI`, `*OPC`, `*ESE`, `*SRE`) goes out without asking. On an instrument with no drives it is sent.
- **One run, one card.** What a run needs set up first goes in its setup, on the same card: an excitation, a laser, and the card's actions it starts once (`isrc.wave_start: run`). Such an action is never auto-approved, and if the run stops, that instrument's outputs go to safe.
- A plan is approved once as a range (if it drives the sample) and then run. Its caution comes from the limits, the approval, ramps and guard readings, so it isn't dry-run first. A dry run on twins is there when someone asks for one (`simulate_plan`, or `dry_run` on a run or a queue), and in auto mode (below). A dry run asked for is only a rehearsal: it never goes on to the real run or starts the queue, and says so (`REHEARSAL ONLY`); the real run is its own call.
- Stopping a plan (the Stop button, the agent's own abort, or a failure) first brings every drive the plan touched back to safe, in each card's safe order, or holds it where the lab config's `safe_actions` says to hold. Only then do the plan's own `on_abort` steps run: they add to going safe and never replace it, and they can't move a drive the lab holds. A guard trip sends that drive back to safe.
- **A rate of 0 is never safe.** Going to safe (a stop, a trip, an interlock, **Hands on the sample**, **Bring to safe**) never sends a drive's ramp rate (a magnet's) to its safe value: at 0 the field would stay where it is, and once stayed at -2.5 T above its interlock. A move whose rate reads 0 is refused at once, saying to set the rate first, instead of waiting for a field that can't move.
- **A stop never hangs on a set point.** After a stop, a set point the instrument ramps to by itself (a cryostat's temperature, a magnet's field) is set and not waited for; the clean-up says it is still ramping there. A temperature set point whose safe value no cryostat reaches (0 K) holds where its reading is instead.
- **Stop is a hard stop wherever it comes from:** the Stop button, the runs pane's **Stop run**, the phone's **Stop**, the sign-in screen's **Stop it**, a chat message that only says stop (`STOP`, `stop!`, `abort`, `/stop`), which stops the lab at once instead of waiting for Kelvin's next step, or Kelvin's `stop_run`, when you ask it to stop in any chat (the record names that chat). No dialog stands in the way, and no stop waits for a lock. A stop also ends the queue (a stopped item always pauses it), declines open approvals, and drops the agent's calls that were still waiting for the lab; a run stopped while its instruments were being checked never starts. An MCP client that cancels a call, or disconnects, gets the same for that call ([agents.md](agents.md)).
- **After a stop, Kelvin says what is still on.** Once the stopped work has finished its safe-stop steps: what reaches the sample with its levels (a laser emitting, its shutter open, a source's output), then a field or a temperature with its reading now. A level whose output is off isn't listed, nor is a drive's rate, range or current limit. It is on a card above the chat with **Bring to safe** and **Dismiss**, in a notice, and in the stopped run's own answer; `stop_run` says it at once when nothing is still stopping (else `lab_status` has it).
- **Bring to safe** (the Lab panel, the card after a Stop) stops what runs, then sets every instrument to the safe values its card declares, in its safe order: outputs off, levels to safe, a field ramped down, a heater off. The lab config's hold is for stops, so it doesn't apply here. As after a stop, a set point is set and not waited for, a temperature no cryostat reaches (0 K) holds where it is, and a drive's rate stays; stages and positioners stay where they are. Pressing it is the approval: nothing asks, a draft card's included. No agent tool reaches it.
- **Stop never waits on an instrument.** A step an instrument holds up (a slow sweep, a reply that doesn't come) ends at once in the chat, and every long wait watches for a stop: a sweep the instrument is still taking (Kelvin then clears the instrument, so a late answer is never taken for another command's), a run's settle or interval, a `stabilize`, a set point the instrument is still ramping to (toward safe too, such as a cryostat cooling down: the set point goes on, the waiting ends), a ramp's step, Kelvin's own wait. A stop that comes while a run is still starting, or between two of its steps, ends it before the next step. A change that finds the lab held says what holds it, at once for a run, the queue or an approval card and after 30 s for anything else, instead of waiting for it. Ramps back to safe are never cut short.
- **Changes go one at a time; readings never come between a run's commands.** Two changes never overlap, from any chat or client, as an approval (a plan's ranges, a queue's) belongs to the whole lab. Each change holds the instruments it uses (a run: those its plan names, with their guards' and interlocks' instruments). A reading from elsewhere (another chat, the Lab panel, the terminal's `get`) reads the other instruments, each with a lock of its own, and gives the latest values of those that are held: a command in between could upset a lock-in's acquisition or a sweep an instrument takes. A change that starts waits for the readings of its instruments already under way to finish. The monitor's watches and interlock checks still read during a run, as they must.
- **Sensing fails closed.** A guard reading that can't be read, or isn't a number, trips the ramp. A guard trip brings the drive back to safe and switches off the output that applies it (a level set to 0 does nothing on a source someone put in current mode), and so does a lab watch's action. An interlock whose condition can't be read refuses its drive. A card can say how to tell a faulty sensor from a reading (`status:` and `plausible:`, [instruments.md](instruments.md#writing-a-card); Kelvin's Lake Shore cards check input A): an open thermometer is then an error, not 0 K.
- **Interlocks are enforced, not only checked.** While an interlock's drive is away from safe, Kelvin reads its condition every few seconds. When it fails, or can't be read, an alarm goes up, the running plan stops (what it stopped says it was the interlock) and the drive goes to safe, even where the lab config holds it on a stop: holding a field the interlock forbids would defeat it. The alarm stays up until the drive is at safe or the condition has held again for a minute, so a sample sitting at the interlock's edge doesn't raise it again and again.
- **Interlocks are foreseen.** Labs write interlocks on the reading (`tc.T_sample < 10`), so Kelvin checks what drives it too: setting the condition, or a set point that drives the reading it names (`tc.setpoint` drives `tc.T_sample`), to a value that fails it while the drive is away from safe is refused, auto mode or not. A set point that takes the reading no further from the condition still goes (cooling while the field comes down). A plan or a queue that would get there, by such a step while its drive is up or by moving its drive while its own set point has the condition failing (a 12 K step at -2.5 T), is refused before it starts; `validate_plan` and a queue's rehearsal say so.
- Approvals come from an `Approver`, a human-facing object the agent never sees.
- **The record says who answered.** Each approval and decline is kept with the person (their account when people sign in), where they answered (the portal, a phone by its name, the approval page) and the time; so is each Stop. Reports print it ("approved by Ada (portal) at 14:02:11 (asked by agent)"), never "agent approved" for a person's yes.
- **Each card says where it acts:** **SIMULATOR** when every instrument it names is simulated (nothing real moves), **REAL HARDWARE** otherwise, on the portal, the phone and the approval page.
- **Each card shows the whole run.** The same card goes to the portal, the phone and the approval page.
  - **First, what you approve.** Each drive away from safe, with its range and its ramp, or the rate the instrument moves it at. That rate is read from the instrument, or taken as the plan sets it (a PPMS's `T_rate`). Its guard and interlock come with it, with the condition's reading now.
  - **Then what else the run changes.** Drives moving toward safe (a cryostat cooling down) or inside the project envelope, and a drive whose output stays off ("won't reach the sample").
  - **An output going on.** The card says what the output applies: its levels set to safe first, or to what the plan sets, and a compliance as it is now. It also says how the instrument is set to apply it (its source function, its waveform). An output once went on into current mode with nothing on its card to say so.
  - **The end.** The card ends with what the run leaves away from safe and what is back at safe.
  - **How long.** The title gives the duration: ramps (each leg of a `ramp:` sweep at its rate, plus its hold), settling (a step's own `settle` where it has one) and waits. It says "at least" when something can't be counted, such as a rate the instrument doesn't give, a stabilize (with `near:`, it may wait out its timeout), readings that take their own time, or readings taken again after a fault (`retry:`). It says "at most" when an `until:` or `while:` may end the run sooner. The card's last lines name both.
  - **What could go wrong.** Each guard with its reading now, and a level whose output is off ("smu.output is off, so smu.V won't reach the sample", said in the result too). What a set point sets off: another drive's guard on the reading it drives while that drive is on, and a lab watch whose range it leaves, with what the watch switches off or the run it stops. A dry run of the same plan or queue that just went wrong, and a queue that goes on after a failed item. A single change whose guard reads past its limit already is refused before anything moves: it would trip at its first step.
  - **Queues and single changes.** A queue's card does the same for the whole queue, in order. A person's own drive change shows its rate and time too.
- **A card in your language says the same.** Cards translated for reading (Settings → Display, [portal.md](portal.md#in-the-portal)) copy every name, number, unit and value from the English; a line whose numbers, names or signs wouldn't all come through stays in English. What you approve, and the record, are the English card.
- **A card says when an instrument it names is out of calibration** (as a person recorded it, [portal.md](portal.md#calibration)), and the record keeps that line with the card. A warning, not a refusal.
- **A change counts only once the instrument shows it.** Before a change, Kelvin reads out what the instrument's error queue already holds, logs it and names it as older, so the change is never blamed for it (and the queue can't overflow). After the change, Kelvin reads the queue again, and for a number it can read back, the value itself. If the instrument clearly shows something else for a moment longer (it refused the value or clipped it, as a generator does above its range for the load setting), the change is reported as failed, with the value the instrument shows. This holds even for a card with no error queue. Switches and modes are not checked this way, since they can take seconds to follow (a laser enabling); nor are instruments that ramp themselves, which Kelvin follows until they arrive. A stage or piezo whose card says how it reports arriving (`busy:` or `arrived:`) is followed until it says it is there, and only then read back. A move Kelvin waits for reads the error queue right after the command too, so a refused move (a stage not homed) fails at once instead of after its whole timeout.
- **Undo is each chat's own.** `undo_last` takes back the latest change this chat made itself (`set_value`, `send_raw`), and only while the value is still what that change left. Never a run's, a queue's or a script's steps, another chat's change or a person's. An undo that moves away from safe asks a person, its card saying what it undoes; auto mode doesn't cover it.
- **When Kelvin isn't sure, it asks.** On a real instrument whose card is a draft (no one has checked it against the instrument), or one Kelvin added to the lab itself (marked `added_by: kelvin`; its limits are still the maker's), every output change away from safe asks a person, and the request says why Kelvin isn't sure. Neither auto mode nor a project envelope covers these changes; a plan or queue a person approved, with that line shown, does. On a draft card every settings change asks too, since its measure and drive tags are unchecked, and so does a change toward safe, since its safe values are unchecked as well (going to safe after a stop, a trip or an alarm never asks). Inside a plan or queue a person approved, its card lists the draft's settings and changes too, and that one approval covers each of its steps. A queue's card covers every value its items set on the draft, even one that is already so when the queue starts, and the settings an item's `on_abort` makes, so a change at the front panel or a stop in the night doesn't ask again. Reading is free. Once a person reviews the limits (**Limits reviewed** in Lab setup) or marks the card reviewed, the usual rules apply.
- **Kelvin may add instruments, not change limits.** When someone names an instrument connected to the computer, Kelvin adds it to the lab config with its card's own limits and the `added_by: kelvin` mark. Only a person tightens limits or removes the mark. An address the scan didn't find is added too (it drives nothing). Every addition is in the event log and the person is told.
- **Switched-off instruments stay off** until a person switches them on. Kelvin can ask (`ask_to_switch_on`, with its reason), and switches one on only on a yes. Switching off stays with people.
- **Only driving the sample waits for a person.** What drives nothing, Kelvin approves itself, whatever the approval mode, and shows in the conversation with why, in the event log too: trying a card on an address the last scan didn't find (it only asks who is there and reads), adding an instrument at such an address (every output change on it still asks until you review its limits), installing a maker's package into one of Kelvin's own environments, removing one no instrument uses, keeping a skill or a recipe (switch it off or delete it in Settings → **Memory**), and saving a file. The exception is a chat that has read text from the web or a file in its folder since you last wrote ([below](#text-from-outside-the-lab)): such text could be steering the model, so you answer. These still ask, since Kelvin's checks can't see what they would do to the sample or they undo your own choice: code on a board or over SSH, removing an environment an instrument uses, and switching an instrument on.
- **Kelvin's skills and recipes steer it later.** A recipe's stages are plans like any other: each goes through the gate (limits, approvals, device protection), a stage that fails its check after its retries ends the recipe, and so does a run that stops.
- **Second opinions only read.** A reviewer's tools look things up in the lab's records; none changes anything, runs a plan or takes a camera frame.
- **Makers' code gets no secrets.** The helper process for a maker's library, and Kelvin's own environments, run without the API keys, tokens and passwords in Kelvin's environment.
- **Claude uses the screen only when you say yes.** With the Claude Code bridge, `operate_computer` hands this computer's screen, mouse and keyboard to Claude for a task in another program; what it changes there bypasses this gate. So a person always approves it first, with the task shown; neither Kelvin itself nor auto mode does.
- **Files Kelvin saves stay in the chat's folder.** In a chat with a folder of its own, Kelvin saves text files there (`write_file`: scripts, notes, tables) without asking, and nowhere else. It never saves a file Windows or Python would run by itself (programs, batch and PowerShell scripts, shortcuts, Python's start-up files). A file it replaces is kept first, in `.kelvin-old` beside it, and every save is in the lab's record. **Kelvin's own folder is out of reach**, wherever the chat's folder is: Kelvin never saves, reads or lists anything in `~/Kelvin` (the lab config, settings, accounts, cards, instrument folders and everyone's chats) or the chats of the person signed in, so it can't rewrite its own limits or read another person's chats; and it never rewrites the lab's records (the event log, the mounted sample's protection, the queue, the catalog, a run's `run.h5`). A folder in `~/Kelvin` can't be chosen for a chat. Python code in a folder an instrument's maker's library loads from (the folder in a `lib://` address) runs on the instrument when it connects, and no card command checks it, so it shows in the conversation whole, approved by Kelvin itself (or by you, after the chat read text from the web).

### Text from outside the lab

Web pages, papers, manuals, makers' example scripts, an instrument's or a board's replies, PDFs a person attaches and files Kelvin reads in a chat's folder can contain text written to steer a model. Kelvin's tools mark such text as outside text (`<outside_text from="...">`), and the models are told it is information, never instructions.

Text from the open web (web pages, web search results, papers) is where such text would come from, so it also pauses auto mode, and so does a file Kelvin reads in the chat's folder (the folder may hold files from anywhere, such as downloaded papers, and Kelvin chose the file, not you): in a chat that has read some, drive changes and runs ask a person until the person writes again in that chat; for an agent through MCP, for 30 minutes. The request says why. An instrument's manual, a maker's files, an instrument's reply and a file a person attached are marked for the model but don't pause auto mode: reading the manual is part of using an instrument well.

### Checked like an experiment, not tested like code

Language models are trained largely on programming, where every step is tested and checked again. At the bench that only costs time, so Kelvin keeps the checks that protect the sample and drops the rest:
- **Instruments are checked before use, not before every scan.** A run checks the instruments it uses: identity, error queue, readings, drives inside limits. An instrument that passed is not asked again for 10 minutes, so a series of scans or a queue doesn't stop to re-check. A plan that drives the sample always checks now, and an instrument whose run failed is checked again next time. **health_check** always checks.
- **No routine dry runs.** Not even a plan that drives the sample is dry-run on its own: what a dry run would predict, the limits, the approval and the guard readings catch on the real run, which goes back to safe if a guard trips. A dry run happens when the person or the agent asks for one, for instance to see how long a long plan or a queue takes, and in auto mode, where no person looks at the run.
- **The agent works like an experimentalist.** Its instructions tell it to:
  - run a plan directly, with no separate validate or simulate first and no test or verification scans;
  - not read a value again to confirm it, since every set and get reports what the instrument says;
  - not try variations to get past a refusal or failure, but stop and say what happened;
  - not write code or re-analyse data unless asked.

### Your lab's own scripts

Kelvin can run one of your lab's measurement scripts as it is (`run_script`), and the script goes through the same gate as Kelvin's own commands:
- **Only from your scripts folder**, the one a person named in Set up. Kelvin never runs a script from anywhere else. The agent can't change the folder, and doesn't edit your script to change a value: a value it was asked to change goes into a copy for that run.
- **One approval for the run.** Kelvin reads the script first, without running it, for the lab instruments it opens and the drive values it uses, and asks a person once, as for a plan. A drive whose values the script works out as it runs, or sets through a driver's own API (`smu.source_voltage = v`), is approved anywhere within its limits, and the request says so (the agent can narrow it with the range it read). A script can't be rehearsed on the simulated lab, so a person approves every run, in auto mode too.
- **Every command through the gate.** The script runs in your lab's own Python with Kelvin's stand-in loaded first. A lab instrument it opens with pyvisa (also through drivers built on it, such as PyMeasure's and QCoDeS's), pyserial or a socket to the instrument's address reaches Kelvin, never the instrument, and Kelvin sends each command through the gate on its own connection. Readings and settings run; drives are checked against the limits, ramped and watched like any other; a change beyond what the person approved asks them, and one beyond the limits is refused.
- **What Kelvin can't check is asked first.** An instrument that isn't in the lab, a Prologix adapter that lab instruments are behind when the script opens it itself to send its `++` commands (its serial port, or a GPIB-ETHERNET's port 1234), and a maker's library that reaches instruments without Kelvin seeing the commands (zhinst, nidaqmx, pythonnet and the like), are named in the approval, or asked about when the script reaches them. A script that reaches hardware through its own DLL calls is beyond what the gate can see.
- **A refusal, a person's no or Stop ends the script.** It gets an error for the refused command (KelvinRefused; for Stop, KeyboardInterrupt), so its own `finally:` still runs. Those clean-up commands go through the gate too, which then lets only moves toward safe through. A script still running 10 s after its last command is ended, with every process it started. Then every drive it touched goes back to safe, or holds where the lab says so, as for a stopped plan; the same happens when it fails with an error. A script that finishes leaves the instruments as it left them, and Kelvin says which drives it left away from safe.
- **Never unwatched.** If Kelvin ends while a script runs, the script ends too. It runs without Kelvin's API keys, tokens and passwords.

### Auto mode

A person can switch approvals from **Ask first** to **Auto for low risk**, in the portal (next to the model picker) or on the approval page. No agent tool can switch it. Kelvin goes back to asking every time it restarts. Auto mode lets everything simple and safe go ahead without asking: small drive changes, runs and queues, and the requests that change nothing on the sample.

In auto mode, a drive change, a run or a queue goes ahead without asking when all of these hold:
- Every drive stays within `auto_fraction` (default 25%) of the way from its safe value to its lab limit. Kelvin judges the target's distance from safe, not the step size, so creeping up in small steps doesn't get around the rule. While a protected device is mounted, a drive it limits also stays within `auto_fraction` of the way to the device's limit, its own auto range included (a gate protected to ±6 V once went to 5 V unasked).
- It sets off nothing that is on: another drive's guard, or a lab watch that would switch something off or stop the run ([the card](#the-rules) lists them). An undo that moves away from safe always asks.
- Switches (outputs, ranges) move at most one step from safe.
- For a run, its dry run finishes cleanly. In auto mode a run is dry-run first, since no person looks at it; a queue is rehearsed as a whole, in order, on simulated copies of the lab's instruments. A run of your lab's own script can't be dry-run, so it always asks.
- The lab config doesn't exclude the instrument.
- Kelvin is sure about the instrument: its card has been reviewed, and if Kelvin added it to the lab, its limits have been reviewed too (see "When Kelvin isn't sure, it asks" above).
- The chat hasn't read text from the web, or a file in its folder, since you last wrote in it ([below](#text-from-outside-the-lab)).

What drives nothing (trying a card, adding an instrument at an address, installs, skills and recipes, saved files) doesn't wait for you in either mode ([above](#the-rules)).

Auto mode never covers what Kelvin can't judge, and these always ask: changes on an instrument Kelvin isn't sure about (a card nobody has checked could be in the wrong units), raw commands it can't classify, running code on a board or over SSH, removing an environment an instrument uses, switching an instrument on, and a second opinion's "stop".

Anything else asks as usual, and the request says why auto mode didn't cover it, and when Kelvin isn't sure about an instrument, what ends that (**Lab** → **Cards…** → **Mark reviewed**, or **Lab** → **Set up…** → **Limits reviewed…**). Each auto-approval shows in the conversation with its reasons and is written to the event log. Limits, interlocks, guard readings and Stop work the same in both modes.

Whoever sets up the lab writes the rule in the lab config:

```yaml
auto_fraction: 0.25                 # share of the way from safe to each lab limit
instruments:
  smu:
    auto: { V: [-5, 5] }            # this parameter's own auto range
  laser:
    auto: never                     # a person approves every change on this instrument
```

### The lab owner's PIN

Everyone at the computer is equal, unless the lab sets an owner's PIN (Settings → **Instruments** → **Lab owner's PIN**, 4 to 12 digits; changing or removing it takes it). Once it is set, what widens Kelvin's reach over the instruments asks for it: widening an instrument's limits or taking one out, saving the lab config (its interlocks and watches), marking limits or a card reviewed, saving a card with a wider limit, safe value or rate than the card it replaces (Kelvin's own, for the lab's version of one), saving a reviewed card with other commands, or deleting a reviewed card, rolling any of these back to a version that widens ([below](#the-lab-configs-history)), turning on auto mode (also on the approval page), unlocking **Hands on the sample**, and lifting or changing a device's protection, including taking a protected device out. Narrowing never does: tightening a limit (**Limits…**), going back to asking, **Hands on the sample**, Stop, **Bring to safe**, protecting a device, a card back to draft. Five wrong tries lock it for five minutes. Kelvin keeps a salted hash of it in `~/Kelvin/owner.json`; no agent tool reaches any of this. On top of the PIN, the owner can give people roles.

### Lab roles

A lab with an owner's PIN can give people roles: **owner**, **operator** and **trainee** (Settings → **Instruments** → **Lab roles**, by an owner signed in, with the PIN). Without roles, Kelvin is as described above.

| | Trainee | Operator | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Read, measure, run what needs no yes; Stop, decline, **Hands on the sample** | yes | yes | yes |
| Say yes to a request: a drive away from safe, a run, a queue, anything Kelvin asks a person | with an operator or the owner, at the card | yes | yes |
| Turn on auto mode | no | with the PIN | with the PIN |
| Change limits, the lab config, a card's limits or review; roll them back; lift a device's protection | no | with the PIN | with the PIN |
| Unlock **Hands on the sample** | with the PIN | with the PIN | with the PIN |
| Change the roles, and where the owner hears of safety changes | no | no | with the PIN |

- **Whoever has no role is a trainee:** an account made after the roles were given, until the owner gives it one.
- **A trainee's yes, confirmed.** When a trainee approves, the card asks who confirms: an operator or the owner picks their name and types their own account's password, or the owner types the PIN. An account without a password can't confirm, since anyone could give its name. Five wrong tries lock it for five minutes. The record and the reports name both: *approved by Cy (portal) with Ben (operator) at 14:02:11*. A no is anyone's. A trainee's phone can decline but not approve; a queue an operator approved at the card before leaving runs the night on that one approval.
- **What a trainee can't do at all**, PIN or not: an operator or the owner signs in to do it. A trainee who learnt the PIN still can't widen the lab.
- **Auto mode** goes back to asking when anyone signs in, and when the owner makes the person signed in a trainee.
- **The PIN stays while there are roles:** give no one a role first, then remove it. At least one person is an owner, and while one is, only an owner changes the roles.
- **Nobody signed in:** Kelvin as an MCP server ([agents.md](agents.md)) has no accounts, so with roles every yes on its approval page takes the owner's PIN, and auto mode stays off there.
- **Not a wall.** The roles are kept in `~/Kelvin/roles.json`. Whoever can edit this computer's files can delete it (the history below still shows what was done); a file Kelvin can't read makes everyone a trainee until the owner gives the roles again.

### The lab config's history

Every change to the lab config (`lab.yaml`: limits, rates, guards, interlocks, watches, auto rules) and to the lab's own cards (their limits, safe values and blocked commands, and which of Kelvin's cards the lab reviewed) is kept as a version: who (the account and its role, and how: the portal, Kelvin itself, the setup), when, why (a line the person may write when saving) and what changed, in words. **widens** marks a change that widened Kelvin's reach: a limit widened or taken out, a rate raised, an interlock, guard or watch taken out or loosened, `auto: never` dropped, `auto_fraction` raised, an instrument added without Kelvin's mark, limits or a card marked reviewed, a card's safe value changed. What the files alone can't judge counts as widening.
- **Where:** Settings → **Instruments** → **Lab config history…**, or **History…** beside the lab config in Lab setup. It filters by what (the lab config, the cards, auto mode and the like), by who, and to what widened. **Diff** shows a version against the one before it.
- **Roll back** puts an earlier version back as a change of its own, kept as a new version that names the one it put back. The lab config is saved and connected as **Save and connect** does, with the PIN; a card needs the PIN when going back widens it. A trainee can't roll back.
- **Edits by hand** (a text editor on `lab.yaml`) are kept as changes made outside Kelvin the next time Kelvin looks: when it starts, and when the lab config, the cards or the history is opened.
- **Also kept:** auto mode turned on, **Hands on the sample** unlocked, the roles, the PIN set or removed, and where the owner hears of safety changes.
- **On disk:** `~/Kelvin/config-history/`, with `audit.jsonl` (a line each) and each version's text. Kelvin only adds to it.

### The owner hears of safety changes

A change that widens Kelvin's reach (above), auto mode turned on, **Hands on the sample** unlocked or the PIN removed goes to the lab owner, whoever made it: *Bench: smu.V: limits widened, [-5, 5] → [-150, 150]*, by whom, when, why, and the version's number.
- **Where:** the owner, signed in, presses **Where my notifications go** (Settings → **Instruments**, with the PIN). Kelvin keeps a copy of their own webhook and email settings (Settings → **Notifications**) for this, readable on this computer, so the notices reach the owner while someone else is signed in. **Stop** forgets the copy.
- Everyone sees them in the app's list, and anyone can tick **Safety changes** among their own notifications.

## Protecting the sample

A finished device (a 2D heterostructure, a nanowire, a fragile flake) is often worth more than the setup. Most are lost to the same few accidents: a gate pushed past its dielectric's breakdown, a ramp that goes on while the leakage climbs, a spike when a source changes range or switches its output under bias, and a discharge while someone rewires. Kelvin guards against each.

**Each device's own limits.** Mount the device in the Lab panel (**Sample**) and press **Protect this device…**. For each gate, give its dielectric and thickness: the limit is 0.3 V/nm for hBN and SiO₂ and 0.2 V/nm for Al₂O₃ and HfO₂, well below breakdown, unless you type your own. Add whatever else the sample can't take, such as a bias current or a laser power. While the device is mounted:
- The tighter of its limits and the lab's holds. No approval, envelope or auto mode passes it, and a refusal names the device.
- Each gate ramps no faster than its rate. It stops and goes back to 0 once its leakage reading passes its limit (1 nA unless you set another).
- The source itself is capped where its card allows. Its range is set to the smallest that covers the limit (even when a smaller one was chosen before, which would clip a sweep the limits allow), with autorange off, and its current limit (compliance) is 10 times the leakage limit, so a breakdown can't pass more. These caps are written into the instrument, so they hold even if Kelvin or the computer stops. They are limits too: nothing raises them while the device is mounted. A cap that can't be written yet waits and is written as soon as it can: when the source is back at 0, the instrument is switched on, or the running plan ends.
- A plan that names another device on the same chip (`device: D5`) is held to both devices' limits for that run.
- Mounting a device, or changing its protection, while a drive sits outside the new limits says so. That drive may then only move back within them, or to safe.

Only a person sets or changes a device's protection. Kelvin reads it (the lab status shows it), and while a protected device is mounted it can't change what is mounted.

**No switching glitches, no clipped sweeps.** A source's range, or its autorange switch, changes only while its level is at safe. The gate refuses it otherwise, whether it comes from a plan, the agent or a raw command. While the range is fixed (autorange off), a level beyond it is refused up front, saying which range it needs, and a plan that would pass it is refused before it starts, unless its setup sets that range first. Outputs already switch cleanly: a drive is parked at safe before its output switches on, and ramped to safe before it switches off.

**A run measures at what you approved.** Parking a level at safe must not leave a run measuring in the dark:
- A plan switches its outputs on (laser emission, RF, a source's output) before the levels they apply, whatever order it writes them in.
- When an output goes on later in a plan or a queue item, the level that plan or item set is ramped back up once the output is on, through every check again.
- After its setup, a run reads every drive it set back from the instrument. If one shows something else (parked, clipped, refused), the run stops before its first point and says which drive. A level lost during the sweep becomes a warning in the run's assessment, which then never says "no problems found".
- Your own `set` outside a plan still parks the level and says so ("V set to 0 V first").

**Hands on the sample.** Before you touch the sample (connect it, rewire, flip a breakout box), press **Hands on the sample** in the Lab panel or on your phone. Kelvin stops what runs and brings everything that applies something to the sample (sources, excitations, light, RF) to safe with its outputs off. Then every drive stays at safe until a person unlocks. Temperature and field stay as they are, and nothing on a stage or a positioner moves, either way (in an optics set-up a stage often carries the sample or an objective): no move, home or raw command, only its own stop. Kelvin can still read and measure. The lock outlasts a restart of Kelvin. To unlock, choose the sample that is in the setup now and confirm it is wired as the next measurement needs and nobody is touching it; its protection holds from that moment. Any paired phone can lock; only one that can approve can unlock.

A source's card names its range and current limit on its level (see [instruments.md](instruments.md#writing-a-card)).

## Watches and notifications

Kelvin keeps an eye on the lab between runs and during them. A **watch** reads one value every few seconds and raises an alarm when the value leaves its range for longer than the hold time. Durations are in lab time. The alarm clears itself when the value comes back. A reading its card flags as a sensor fault (an open thermometer) alarms at once and acts as an out-of-range one does, since the value could be anything. Kelvin also raises an alarm when a real instrument reports an error in its own error queue (checked once a minute), and when an interlock fails while its drive is away from safe ([above](#the-rules)). A lab watch that sends a level to safe also switches off the output that applies it.

**An instrument that stops answering** raises one alarm in plain words, whoever met it (a watch, a run, a set, Kelvin's own check), naming the instrument, its bus and what to check: *nvm (Keithley 2182 / 2182A nanovoltmeter) doesn't answer on GPIB (address 7): cable or power?* Through a maker's software it names that: *ppms (…) doesn't answer through MultiVu at 127.0.0.1:5000: is MultiVu running, with its server started?* The raw code stays at the end in brackets. It goes up at once when the bus says the instrument can't be reached at all (nobody listening on GPIB, a pulled USB cable), after three failures in a row when it is only slow, and at once when the failure ended a queue item. It clears when the instrument answers again, saying how long it was out.
- **While a run or queue item goes,** every real instrument is asked to answer every 20 s (its identity on the bus; for a maker's software, its headline reading), so one nothing reads, such as a voltmeter during a cool-down, is noticed too.
- **A power cut resets an instrument.** An output the running plan switched on that reads off twice, 5 s apart, raises one alarm and stops the plan, which would otherwise go on measuring with it off.
- **Garbled replies are one comms fault.** A reading that read before and now can't be read, an error-queue reply that isn't one, or a reply with characters no instrument sends where its identity or error queue should be, is said as garbled, never as an instrument error or a value: *nvm's reply to :READ? arrived garbled (…): a loose cable, a bad adapter or electrical noise*. A run stops at such a reply. One alarm goes up at the first and stays until the replies have been clean for 5 minutes; meanwhile the instrument's error-queue alarms wait.

**The set-point watchdog.** Every set point Kelvin sends to an instrument that ramps to it by itself (a temperature, a field, a position) is followed until its reading gets there. When the reading stops getting closer for 5 minutes, an alarm goes up and people are told: the instrument may be stuck (its rate, its helium, a heater or a compressor). A slow ramp still counts as getting closer. A lab at rest is never followed, nor a set point whose switch is off (a controller's heater range at off) until it is on. The alarm clears when the reading moves on or gets there, or the set point changes. A wait for such a set point (in `set_value`, a plan's setup or a stepped sweep) ends after 10 minutes without progress, saying so, instead of waiting out the card's timeout (4 h on a PPMS); in a run it then stops the run, as Stop does. A move away from safe that a wait gives up on (no progress, or its time is up) is held where its reading got to, as Stop leaves it: its guards are watched only while Kelvin waits. A plan's `ramp:` sweep stops after its own `stall:` time ([data.md](data.md#measuring-while-it-ramps)).

Who can add a watch, and what it may do:

| Watch | Added by | On alarm |
|---|---|---|
| Lab watch | A person, in the lab config | Tells people; can stop the running plan and send named drives to their **safe** values (the config refuses any other value) |
| Your watch | You, in the Lab panel (**Watches → Add…**) | Tells people |
| Kelvin's watch | The agent (`watch`, `watches`, `unwatch`), e.g. "tell me if the sample warms above 4 K while this runs" | Tells people |

Lab watches can't be removed from the portal or by the agent; they change only in the lab config.

**Watches and alarms outlast a restart.** Your watches and Kelvin's are kept for each lab in `watches.json` in the data folder, next to the queue, and the latest alarms in `alarms.json`. When Kelvin starts again (an update, a crash, a power cut), when the lab is connected again, or when you sign in, they come back. One whose reading the lab no longer has is dropped and said so. An alarm that was on when Kelvin stopped shows as ended by the stop; its watch raises it again if it still holds.

**After a restart, Kelvin says what the stop left behind.** Once the instruments are connected, a card above the chat (and the agent's `lab_status`) lists what reaches the sample and was left on (a source's output, a laser, RF), runs it cut short (kept up to their last point), the queue item it was running, alarms that were on, and watches it couldn't bring back; with them, temperature, field and positions as they were. **Bring the outputs to safe** does that, as **Hands on the sample** would (temperature, field and positions stay as they are); **Dismiss** closes the card. When the work it cut short ran unattended (a queue), the outputs go to safe at once and the card says so.

**Light left on with nothing happening goes to safe.** When nothing has happened for an hour (no command, run or queue: a turn that died, a usage limit, a person who left), Kelvin brings lasers and light sources to safe, records it and tells you. Other outputs (sources, excitations, RF) it only tells you about, since a gate may be held on purpose, unless you tick them too. Temperature, field and positions stay. Settings → **Notifications** → **Switch off outputs left idle** sets the time (15 minutes to 4 hours) or turns it off.

```yaml
watch:
  - read: tc.T_sample
    above: 20
    for: 10                          # seconds it must stay above before the alarm
    then: { laser.emission: off }    # only ever toward safe; also stops the running plan
    note: The sample must stay cold; the laser is the usual culprit.
```

**How Kelvin tells you.** Alarms show as a banner above the chat until you press **Got it**, and the window title starts with (!). In Settings → Notifications:
- **Allow on this computer** turns on system notifications in your browser: alarms always, and approvals waiting and finished runs while Kelvin is in the background.
- **Also send to** takes a webhook: an [ntfy](https://ntfy.sh) topic (push to your phone with the ntfy app), or a Slack, Teams or Discord incoming webhook. Tick which notices go there (alarms and finished runs by default), then press **Send a test**. The address is kept like an API key and never shown again. Nothing leaves the computer unless you set one; when you do, the notices carry lab details, so use a private topic or channel.
- **Also send by email** takes your institution's or provider's mail server (SMTP, with STARTTLS, SSL or neither), an account and the addresses to send to. The same notices you tick go there, which reaches a phone with no other service. It is off until a server and an address are set. The password is kept like an API key, never shown, and never written into an error or a log.
- **Heartbeat** takes an address from a service that alerts when pings stop ([healthchecks.io](https://healthchecks.io), an Uptime Kuma push monitor). While Kelvin runs and its monitor watches the lab, it pings that address every few minutes (1 to 60) and sends it nothing else. If Kelvin or the computer stops in the night, the pings stop and that service tells you.
- **When nobody answers a request at this computer**, after its wait (5 minutes) it goes to people away from it as an alarm: the Kelvin app on a paired phone, the email and the webhook (with alarms ticked). It then waits up to an hour more (15 minutes to 12 hours, or **Decline it after 5 minutes** as before). A phone allowed to control approves it there. Nothing moves while it waits, and a queue waits on it. With no way to reach anyone away from the computer, it is declined after 5 minutes, and the record says so.
